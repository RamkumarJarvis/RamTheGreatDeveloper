## 1. Existing consumer products that attach to an existing mirror or sit against it

### Takeaway
No shipping product does what this kit proposes: stick a screen and camera on the mirror you already own and try on your own clothes. The pieces exist separately, though:
- **Phone mounts for mirrors** (US$12–24 retail; a 2024–25 Kickstarter with 398 backers). These show people already stick phones on mirrors to work out and watch.
- **Touch switches and touch-LED kits for bathroom mirrors**, a common product in India at about ₹2,500 for a touch LED mirror. These show touch-on-mirror is familiar.
- **Displays built to stick to surfaces:** Displace's suction TV and its 2026 suction hub for existing TVs, and espresso's 5.3 mm magnetic-mount displays.
- **Chinese 21.5–32" Android "magic mirror" touch panels** at US$85–500.
- **Body tracking added to existing screens:** Peloton Guide (an add-on camera box, discontinued July 2025) and cult.fit's Energy Meter (phone camera only).

I found **no 2024–2026 crowdfunding campaign for an add-on smart-mirror kit**. Smart-mirror campaigns cluster in 2016–2019 and were all full mirrors.

### Inferences
- **The white space is real but must beat a ₹0–1,000 phone mount.**
  - People already put phones on mirrors with suction or magnets for fitness and video (MirrorPlay, MirrorFone, ESR). Tier 1 therefore competes with very cheap generic mounts; its value has to be the software.
  - Peloton Guide's failure (US$495 → US$195 → discontinued) is a warning: an add-on camera box for an existing screen does not sell on hardware alone.
  - cult.fit shows Indian users accept on-device, consent-based camera tracking when there is a clear benefit.
- **Mounting precedents.** Displace (suction mounting on glass and walls, plus a battery-powered suction hub) and espresso (thin panel plus magnetic quick-release mount) are the clearest examples of a display that "sticks" to a surface. The pattern that transfers to this kit is a **thin, light (<1 kg) screen on a separately bonded magnetic dock**.
- **Touch on a mirror is normal in India** through ₹2,500 touch LED mirrors. "Tap the mirror" is not a new behaviour to teach; the kit's touch, however, will be on the screen itself, not through the glass.
- **Where the kit is unique.** Full smart mirrors (HEYMIRROR, Qualcomm demo) and display mirrors in India (₹30,000+) are the "replace the mirror" alternative. The kit's pitch is to keep your mirror and add a screen for under ₹15,000.

## 2. Thin screens that could be stuck onto part of a mirror, and which give a usable full-body preview in portrait

### Takeaway
- **15.6" USB-C touch portable monitors** are the practical sweet spot. They cost about **₹6,000–14,000 in India** (most ₹9,900–12,200), weigh about 0.85–1.0 kg, are 5–20 mm thick, give 200–300 nits, and draw about 6–11 W.
- **Android tablets** (11", ₹11,500–20,000; 13", about ₹50,000) combine screen, camera and compute, but their screens are small.
- **Flexible OLED** costs US$600–1,340 even at 13.3", and the **LG W6 "Wallpaper" TV** (9 mm, US$5,500+) is far too big and costly for this use.

By my geometry, from 1.5–2 m away, a portrait 15.6" screen shows the body at about **37%** of the reflection's apparent height, 21.5" about **51%**, 27" about **64%** and 32" about **76%**. An 11" tablet is about **26%** and a phone about **17%**. So phones and tablets work for **capture and close-up review**, 15.6" works as a "second mirror" preview, and 21.5–27" begins to feel life-like.

## 4. Compute: can a mid-range Android phone (or tablet) be the brain at 30 fps, vs a Raspberry Pi 5 box, vs a cloud API?

**Yes for body tracking.** MediaPipe BlazePose ran at 20 ms (Lite) and 25 ms (Full) per frame on a **2018 Pixel 3 GPU**, about 40–50 fps, and Google's ML Kit Pose ran about 30 fps on a Pixel 3 XL. A 2024–26 mid-range phone should match or exceed this, but no measured mid-range figure was found.

**Photo-real try-on is a cloud job** at about US$0.06–0.075 (₹6–7) per image, taking seconds.

## 5. Mounting on mirrors: VHB, suction, clips, magnets; backing damage; weight limits; swinging doors; bathrooms

### Takeaway
- **Adhesive (VHB) is the right primary bond on clean glass, but only for light loads.** 3M's static design rule is **0.25 psi, i.e. 4 in² of tape per lb**, which works out to about **57 cm² per kg** with a safety factor of more than 5. A 1 kg screen therefore needs only about 57 cm² of tape. A 5–6.5 kg 21.5–27" panel needs 284–370 cm², and its load then passes through the mirror's own fixing, so it should be mechanically supported instead.
- **Suction cups leak and are temperature-sensitive.** They are fine for a phone, not for a screen. Displace's suction-mounted TVs and battery-powered hub suggest a powered design, but how its mechanism works was not verified.
- **The silver backing is on the back of the mirror**, so tape on the front glass does not touch it. Damage comes from solvent-based adhesives and acetoxy silicones on the backing.
- **Heat is a minor issue:** an 8–15 W panel at about 40–48 °C is well under the ΔT of about 35 °C that cracks cut-edge float glass.
- **Steel almirahs are magnetic** (CRCA steel), which allows mounting with no adhesive at all.
- **Wardrobe hinges** are specified for 4–6 kg doors (2 hinges) or 6–12 kg (3 hinges), so add at most about 1 kg.
- **Bathrooms are a poor first target:** humidity, zone rules and no IP rating on consumer screens.

### Cited Findings

#### Adhesives
- **3M VHB design values:** long-term static loads (dead weight) use a 0.25 psi design strength, i.e. 4 in² of tape per 1 lb, with a safety factor of more than 5. Dynamic loads (e.g. wind) use 12 psi (85 kPa). The foam "will tend to show creep" under long-term stress ([3M VHB Tape Design Guide](https://multimedia.3m.com/mws/media/1400379O/3m-vhb-tape-design-guide.pdf); [3M VHB Structural Glazing Tapes B23F/G23F TDS](https://multimedia.3m.com/mws/media/411785O/3m-vhb-structural-glazing-tapes-b23f-and-g23f-technical-data-sheet.pdf)).
- **3M VHB 4910** (clear, 1.0 mm acrylic foam): long-term to 93 °C, short-term to 149 °C. The closed-cell foam "seals against water and moisture". These are marketing claims; no humidity-ageing data was found ([3M VHB 4910](https://www.3m.com/3M/en_US/p/d/v000080046/); [3M HR page](https://www.3m.com/3M/hr_HR/p/d/b40072022/); [Dillon Supply](https://www.dillonsupply.com/Product/3m-021200-64588)).
- **VHB price in India:** 3M VHB 4910, 12 mm × 11 m, at ₹1,069–1,224 per roll, undated ([IndiaMART](https://m.indiamart.com/items/3m-vhb-tape-4910-s259637.html)); a "TapeCase 3M VHB 4910" at ₹1,567 on Amazon, per a 19 Jun 2026 affiliate post ([MagicDrop](https://magicdrop.in/drops/3m-double-sided-tape)).
- **Removing VHB from glass** (forums and consumer guides): saw through the foam with fishing line or floss, warm it with a heat gun, dissolve the residue with citrus remover, IPA, WD-40 or Goo Gone, and finish with a plastic scraper or 3M's Stripe Off Wheel. #### Suction cups
- Holding comes from a pressure difference. Lifetime is set by air leaking in, which depends strongly on the cup's volume, stiffness and elastic modulus. In a smooth-surface test the cup held about 9 kg, showing no complete vacuum is reached ([arXiv 1906.01459](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.01459); [APS abstract](https://meetings-archive.aps.org/mar/2019/r58/13)).
- Consumer guides add that residue, dust, cleaner surfactants, a warped lip and temperature swings (cold stiffens the rubber; heat expands trapped air) break the seal. Suction is "a temporary state of physics, not a permanent mounting solution". These are lower-quality sources ([The Tools Trunk](https://thetoolstrunk.com/?p=11963); [EngineerFix](https://engineerfix.com/how-do-you-make-suction-cups-stick/); [Alibaba tips](https://carinterior.alibaba.com/tips/winter-vacuum-holder-fail)).
- Displace's TVs use suction mounting on drywall, textured walls and glass (Section 1). Details of its powered or active mechanism were not retrievable.

#### Magnets
- Published pull force assumes a thick, flat steel plate; thinner steel reduces it ([K&J Magnetics](https://www.kjmagnetics.com/blog/steel-thickness-and-magnetic-fields)).
- One Polymagnet datasheet shows 13.2 lb attraction centred, falling to 2.8 lb attraction (5.9 lb shear) when offset by 0.2 in ([Polymagnet datasheet](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0874/1824/1303/files/1002270_datasheet.pdf)).
- "Shear" is the downward load a magnetic hook can hold ([Gator Magnetics](https://gatormagnetics.com/blogs/gator-bites/why-magnetic-hooks-dont-work)).
- No general shear-to-pull ratio was found.
- Godrej Interio steel almirahs are sold as "CRCA steel" (cold-rolled steel) ([Bajaj Finserv listing](https://www.bajajfinserv.in/hindi/bmall/godrej-interio-2-door-crca-steel-almirah-wardrobe-30161803sd01019-blush-red/p/98364)).

#### Mirror backing and adhesives
- General construction adhesives containing solvents such as toluene or xylene can pass through the backing paint and attack the silver, causing "black spots" or desilvering.
- Acetoxy silicones release acetic acid as they cure. Mirror mastic or neutral-cure silicone is recommended; a neutral-cure datasheet says it is "non-corrosive to metals, mirror backings".
- These are vendor, DIY and forum sources ([EngineerFix](https://engineerfix.com/how-to-glue-a-mirror-to-a-wall-safely-2/); [Hranipex neutral mirror silicone](https://www.hranipex.com/en/product/h-silicone-mirror-transparent-neutral-silicone-sil1/); [Bostik mirror sealant TDS](https://diy.bostik.com/sites/default/files/2022-08/Bostik-DIY-South-Africa-Sealants-Mirror-Technical-Data-Sheet.pdf); [Warm Glass forum](https://warmglass.com/phpBB3/viewtopic.php?p=10987)).
- Some products are unsuitable for vinyl safety backing or acrylic mirrors ([Fine Homebuilding forum](https://finehomebuilding.com/forum/adhesive-for-mirrored-plex)).

#### Heat and glass
- Thermal stress in float glass is about 0.63 MPa per °C of centre-to-edge temperature difference (Viridian: 0.62).
  - The IStructE limits cited by Saint-Gobain for float glass ≤12 mm are a ΔT of 35 °C (as-cut or arrissed edges), 40 °C (smooth-ground) and 45 °C (polished).
  - Lab tests broke 4–12 mm float glass at ΔT of 30–35 °C up to 55–60 °C.
  - Annealed glass is more vulnerable than tempered ([Saint-Gobain](https://www.saint-gobain-glass.co.uk/document/the-resistance-of-glass-to-thermal-stress); [Viridian](https://www.viridianglass.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Viridian-TechDirect™-Thermal-Stress-Glass-Strength.pdf); [Frontiers in Thermal Engineering, 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/thermal-engineering/articles/10.3389/fther.2024.1488206/pdf); [Building Connection](https://buildingconnection.com.au/2016/07/29/how-to-avoid-thermal-breakage/)).
- **Device surface temperatures:**
  - A fanless Surface Pro 7 averaged about 42 °C on its back after 30 minutes of heavy load, with spots near 48 °C ([Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Should-tablets-have-fans-Analyzing-tablet-PC-performance.446073.0.html)).
  - An LG G Pad 10.1 peaked at 40 °C ([Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck-cn.com/LG-G-Pad-10-1-V700.129342.0.html)).
  - Portable monitors convert their 8–15 W draw to heat ([KTC](https://us.ktcplay.com/blogs/support-tips/portable-monitor-overheating-in-sunlight)).
- **Touch-temperature limits** (via companion notes): IEC 62368-1 TS1 limits for parts a user touches follow ISO 13732-1 ([UL tech brief](https://japan.ul.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/27/2014/06/1_techbrief_touchtemp.pdf)).

#### Wardrobe doors
- **Blum:** two hinges are optimal for doors of 4–6 kg and three for 6–12 kg, for doors up to 600 mm wide; doors up to 650 mm wide need an extra hinge. Weigh the door including handles ([Blum](https://ea.blum.com/en/number-of-hinges/)).
- **Another European concealed-hinge sheet:** 2 hinges up to 20 lb, 3 hinges for 20–40 lb, for doors up to 24" wide. These recommendations exclude accessory attachments "such as mirrors and racks" ([Lowe's PDF](https://pdf.lowes.com/productdocuments/3a6f24c9-c094-4807-94b1-ca197bdb3974/65119808.pdf)).

#### Bathrooms
- IEC 60364-7-701 zone summaries (secondary sources; national versions differ):
  - **Zone 0** (inside the bath or shower): SELV only, ≤12 V AC or ≤30 V DC, with the source outside zones 0–2.
  - **Zones 1 and 2:** at least IPX4. Zone 2 extends 0.6 m horizontally, up to 2.25 m high.
  - All circuits need 30 mA RCD protection.
  - UK guidance keeps sockets other than SELV or shaver units at least 3 m from zone 1.
  - Sources: [Schneider Electrical Installation Wiki](https://www.electrical-installation.org/enwiki/Bathroom_electrical_installation); [SELECT guide](https://select.org.uk/common/uploaded%20files/Technical/FreeDownloads/30269_PG_BathOrShower.pdf); [WireSketch](https://vanmoofer.com/wiresketch/learn/iec-60364-wet-room-zones/); [Open Exam Prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/uk-am2-assessment/building-regs-special-locations/special-locations-bathrooms).
- An India-specific bathroom electrical guide exists but was not read ([StudioMatrx, Jul 2026](https://www.studiomatrx.org/guides/bathroom-electrical-code-india)).

### Inferences
- **VHB area needed (3M static rule; my calculation).** At 56.9 cm² per kg:

| Load | Tape area | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Phone, 0.25 kg | about 14 cm² | |
| 15.6" monitor, 1.0 kg | about 57 cm² | four 25 × 60 mm pads |
| Tablet in case, 1.3 kg | about 74 cm² | |
| 21.5" panel, 5 kg | about 284 cm² | |
| 27" panel, 6.5 kg | about 370 cm² | |

  The 12 psi dynamic value (0.84 kgf/cm²) means 57 cm² resists about 48 kgf of short shock, such as a door slam. Static creep, not shock, is what limits the design.
- **Recommended mounting architecture ("dock plate").** Bond a thin steel or aluminium plate (or a MagSafe-style ring for phones) to the glass, using VHB on IPA-cleaned glass. The screen or tablet then attaches magnetically, with a mechanical lip or hook to carry the shear load and a short safety tether.
  - The user can lift the screen off to charge it or use it elsewhere, which also matches the espresso and MagSafe user model.
  - VHB is effectively permanent; removal needs line-sawing and solvents. Ship a removal tool and spare pads, and state it plainly.
  - For renters, offer a Command-strip variant rated at least 4× the device weight, a hook over the top edge of the door or frame, or a magnetic variant.
- **Mount by mirror type** (design inferences):
  - **(a) Steel almirah (Godrej-type CRCA):** magnets go directly on the steel door around the mirror, with no adhesive on the glass.
  - **(b) Wooden or laminate wardrobe door:** screw or VHB the dock to the door panel beside or above the mirror rather than to the glass. Keep the added mass ≤1 kg, given 4–6 kg hinge budgets that exclude mirrors and accessories.
  - **(c) Wall mirror:** dock on the glass for screens ≤1.3 kg. For a heavier screen, use a wall cleat or an over-the-top hook so the mirror's own adhesive or clips never carry the screen.
  - **(d) Acrylic "mirror stickers" and thin stick-on mirrors:** do not load them at all.
- **Silver backing.** A front-mounted kit does not touch the silvering. The only risks are solvent residue creeping round the edge to the back during removal (use IPA or citrus, not strong solvents, near the edges) and acrylic mirrors.
- **Heat.** A panel or tablet back at about 40–48 °C on room-temperature glass (25–32 °C in Indian homes) gives a local ΔT of roughly 10–20 °C. That is under the 35 °C as-cut-edge limit, but avoid direct sun on the mirror and leave the 1 mm VHB air gap or more.
- **Swinging doors.** The weak point is the cable, not the bond. Options in order of simplicity:
  1. Run the screen on its own battery (tablet) and charge it off the door.
  2. Use a coiled or spring USB-C lead with a service loop on the hinge side, clipped to the door.
  3. Use a BIS-certified power bank on the door. This adds 300–450 g and lithium risk.

  Mains never goes on the door.
- **Bathrooms.** Consumer tablets and portable monitors found here carry no IP rating, and bathrooms add condensation and zone rules. Market tier 1 (phone only, phone removed after use) for bathrooms, and tiers 2 and 3 for bedroom and wardrobe mirrors.

### Gaps
- The 3M VHB design guide could not be opened directly; the 4 in²/lb and 12 psi figures come from search extracts of 3M documents. No 3M data was found on humidity ageing, bonding to the plastic backs of portable monitors, or adhesion after condensation.
- No source quantified heat damage to the silvering at 40–50 °C, or ammonia-cleaner edge corrosion.
- No standard shear-to-pull ratio for magnets on painted CRCA steel, and no thickness for Godrej door sheet steel, was found.
- No load data was found for Indian stick-on acrylic mirrors, nor weight ratings for typical almirah or wardrobe mirror fixings (clips, mastic).
- How Displace's active suction works and how it fails (power loss, leakage) is undocumented in the sources found.

## 6. Typical Indian mirror sizes and types in homes, and what screen size fits

### Takeaway
There is no Indian standard; sizes come from retail listings.
- **Bathroom mirrors:** small, 12×18" to 15×18" pieces; vanity mirrors about 24×30–36".
- **Wall mirrors:** 24×42" or 30×30".
- **Full-length mirrors:** commonly 5 ft (1,500 mm) or 900×1,800 mm.
- **Dressing-table mirrors:** e.g. 80×60 cm on a 120×45×75 cm table.

## 7. Safety: low-voltage power, certified adapters, power banks, falling objects, heat, children

### Takeaway
- Keep **only USB-C low voltage on the mirror.** USB PD's standard profiles top out at 20 V (background knowledge, not verified in this session), well under the 60 V DC limit of IEC 62368-1's touch-safe ES1 class. Supply it from a **BIS CRS-registered adapter** at the wall: 65 W GaN units cost ₹1,400–2,500 in India. Power adapters and power banks are compulsory CRS categories, and the safety standard is migrating to IS/IEC 62368-1:2023, with the old standards accepted until 1 Nov 2028.
- **The biggest physical risk is a falling screen near children.** In the US, furniture, TV and appliance tip-overs injure about 17,800 people a year, 44% of them children, and TVs were involved in 47% of tip-over deaths. Keep stuck-on mass ≤1–1.3 kg, add a tether, and mechanically support anything heavier.
- **Heat on the glass is low risk** at 40–48 °C surfaces (Section 5).

### Inferences
- **Power design.**
  - Ship (or require) a BIS-registered USB-C PD adapter (≥30 W for a 15.6" monitor plus webcam; 65 W if a Pi box is also powered) that stays at the wall socket. Only a 1.5–3 m USB-C cable runs to the mirror, so the kit itself carries no mains.
  - Check the adapter's R-number on the BIS CRS portal and print it in the manual.
  - Background, not verified in this session: USB PD SPR profiles are ≤20 V and EPR ≤48 V, both under the ES1 limit of 60 V DC.
- **Power banks.** Allow a power bank only as a door-mounted option for swinging doors. It must be BIS-listed, never stuck directly to the glass in sun, and the kit should not sell its own lithium pack in v1, to avoid CRS and transport overhead.
- **Mass limits (design rules).**
  - Adhesive or magnetic docks on glass: ≤1.3 kg device mass, a ≥4× static safety margin on removable strips (VHB per 3M's own rule), and a mandatory secondary tether to the door, wall or frame.
  - Anything ≥2 kg (tier 3): hook, cleat or floor-stand support only.
  - A 1 kg screen falling 1.5 m onto a toddler is the realistic worst case, comparable to the TV tip-over pattern at a much smaller mass.
- **Child safety.**
  - Keep cables short and clipped to avoid a loop near the floor, and avoid button-cell remotes. The companion notes cover Reese's Law (US) for button cells.
  - Use rounded bumpers on the screen's corners.
  - Give the camera a physical shutter and an LED indicator, and run a parental mode that blocks cloud uploads by default.
- **Heat.** Use auto-dim and sleep when no one is in front of the mirror to cut both heat and power. Warn against direct sunlight on the mirror.

### Inferences
- **Anchors** (my judgment):
  - Tier 1 hardware at ₹499–999 sits with ring lights and phone mounts, so it is an easy impulse buy if the app is good.
  - Tier 2 at about ₹12,999–15,999 sits next to the Echo Show 5 (₹10,999) and a Redmi Pad 2 (₹13,999). Users will compare it with "just buying a tablet", so it must visibly do something a tablet on a stand cannot: mirror docking, an auto-capture full-body view and a wardrobe pipeline.
  - Tier 3 at ₹22,000–52,000 overlaps the cheapest display smart mirrors (about ₹30,000) and 32" TVs. It is viable only as a premium SKU.
- **Deal-driven market.** India's heavy discounting (₹275 ring lights, ₹98 suction cases, a ₹3,499 21.5" monitor at "73% off" MRP) means MRPs are not meaningful. Plan around street prices and sale events.
- **Subscription sensitivity.** With cloud renders costing ₹6–7 each, a free tier with on-device live overlay and a few renders a month, plus paid render packs, fits better than a high monthly fee. Willingness-to-pay data for India was not researched here; see the companion market notes.
