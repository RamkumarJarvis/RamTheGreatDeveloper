## 1. Transparent LED film (self-adhesive "LED film screen")

### Takeaway
LED film can physically be stuck onto the front of a mirror and is bright enough, but it cannot show a convincing try-on image.

- **Resolution:** the finest film pitch on offer is about P2.5, and only a few vendors claim it. P3.91 is the finest pitch commonly listed. That gives at most ~80,000 pixels over 0.5 m × 1.0 m (about QVGA) and a clearly visible dot grid at 1.5 m.
- **LG:** LG's own film (LTAK140/LAT140) is P13.7, which is only ~2,600 pixels over that area.
- **On a mirror:** the film would also dim the reflection to T² (about 28–90% depending on the film), show a doubled grid, and could only add light.

It is viable for a coarse UI (icons, large text, colour swatches), not for a photographic try-on image.

**Pixels across 0.5 m × 1.0 m** (my calculations: 500 mm ÷ pitch by 1000 mm ÷ pitch)

| P3.91 (common) | 128 × 256 | ~32,800 | 9.0 arcmin | 13.4 m |
| P5 | 100 × 200 | 20,000 | 11.5 arcmin | 17.2 m |

| P20 | 25 × 50 | 1,250 | 45.8 arcmin | 68.8 m |

- **Visible grid:** 20/20 acuity is about 1 arcmin, which is 0.44 mm at 1.5 m. Even P2.5 film is about 6× coarser, so the LED dot grid would be plainly visible at mirror distance. Even the most lenient "1 m per mm" rule would need about P1.5 or finer for 1.5 m viewing, and no film that fine is sold.
- **Recognisable person in an outfit at 1.5 m:** suppose the film shows a full-height image of a person 1.0 m tall. A face is about 1/8 of body height.
  - P2.5: 400 px tall, face about 50 px.
  - P3.91: 256 px tall, face about 32 px.
  - P6.25: 160 px tall, face about 20 px.
  - P13.7: 73 px tall, face about 9 px.

  My judgment of what each conveys (no source was found for face or garment recognisability thresholds):
  - P2.5–P3.91 can convey a recognisable outfit (garment type, colour blocks, coarse pattern) at "scoreboard" quality, with visible pixels.
  - P6.25 and coarser show only a silhouette and colour blocks.
  - LG's P13.7 can show only icons or large text.
  - Fine fabric patterns and texture will not survive at any film pitch.
- **When aligned to the user's reflection** (see Q7), the reflection occupies the glass at half life-size. A torso garment (about 0.6 m in reality) then covers only about 0.3 m of film: 120 px tall at P2.5, 77 px at P3.91, 48 px at P6.25 and 22 px at P13.7.
- **Power and heat for a 0.5 m² patch at full brightness:**
  - Vendor figures of 100–360 W/m² average and 300–800 W/m² max imply about 50–180 W average and 150–400 W max.
  - LG LAT140 works out to about 115 W/m² (37 W per 0.322 m² panel including bezels), plus a 20 W controller. LTAK140 is about 233 W/m².
  - At 5 V (3CINNO), 150 W means about 30 A and 300 W about 60 A, so heavy cabling and connectors are needed.
  - Indoors at 1–1.5 m, 1,000–4,000 nits is far more than needed, so in practice the film would run heavily dimmed, which cuts power roughly in proportion.
  - Rough surface-heating estimate, assuming about 10–15 W/m²·K combined convection and radiation:
    - 200–360 W/m²: about 15–35 °C above ambient;
    - about 115 W/m²: about 8–12 °C;
    - dimmed to ≤50 W/m²: a few °C.
  - Treat these as order-of-magnitude only.
- **Weight and thickness:**
  - Chinese film is 1.2–3 mm thick, and 0.5 m² weighs about 0.5–1.5 kg at the claimed 1–3 kg/m².
  - LG needs two 655 × 492 mm panels (about 0.49 × 1.31 m in portrait), about 2 kg, plus bezels 25 mm deep and a 1.5 kg controller. LG's product is therefore not a bare "sticker".
- **How it would look on a mirror** (no source found; physical reasoning):
  - It must go on the front surface. The silvering is on the back and is usually painted over.
  - The reflection passes through the film twice (in, and back out), so reflected brightness scales with T²:
    - LAT140 at 53%: about 28% of the original;
    - LTAK140-GU at 60%: about 36%;
    - vendor 85% claims: about 72%;
    - vendor 90–95% claims: about 81–90%.
  - Vendor "transparency" is often an open-area figure, so the real optical loss may be higher.
  - The film's traces, LED packages and driver ICs are seen twice: directly, and as a mirror image about 2t/n behind the film. For typical 4–6 mm mirror glass (my assumption) with n ≈ 1.5, that is 5–8 mm. This "double grid" shifts with head movement, so expect parallax doubling, possible moiré, and a screen-door look over the reflection at 2.5–14 mm spacing.
  - **Unresolved orientation risk:** street-facing window film is applied to the inside of the glass and viewed through it. If a given film emits toward its adhesive side, sticking it on a mirror would fire the LEDs into the mirror. The image would then be seen only as a doubled reflection (about 4% from the front surface plus the silver), with the backs of the LEDs and ICs facing the user. Vendors must confirm whether front-view variants exist.
  - The glossy protective film adds a third reflecting surface, which creates ghost reflections of room lights.

### Inferences
- **Reflection brightness:** over a mirror, the reflection passes through the panel twice:
  - 43% transparency: about 18% of the original brightness;
  - 38%: about 14%;

  The mirror behind becomes very dark.

## 3. Projection overlays (holographic / transparent projection film + short-throw or pico projector)

### Takeaway
Projection onto a mirror does not form a usable image.

- **On the bare mirror:** the silvered surface reflects the beam specularly, back toward the projector or onto the room and user. The result is hotspots plus "ghost" doubles from the glass.
- **With clear holographic film:** the film is cheap but low-gain and designed for rear projection. It washes out unless the room is near-dark.
- **With a diffusing layer:** a diffusing layer, such as PDLC switched to opaque, can take a projection, but only by hiding the mirror in that area.

### Cited Findings
- **Holographic rear-projection film prices and specs:**

  - Holographic film gain is about 0.8–1.2.
  - With a 4,000-lumen short-throw projector, it "may appear dim unless ambient is near-zero".
  - Contrast is low in ambient light.
  - Source: [Alibaba guide](https://electronics.alibaba.com/buyingguides/rear-projection-film-for-glass-how-to-choose-right)
- **Projector aimed at a mirror:** "If you aim straight at it, the light will just be thrown back onto the projector itself." What you see depends on viewing angle; you can tilt the mirror to throw the image elsewhere. — [Linus Tech Tips forum](https://linustechtips.com/topic/1574151-projector-projecting-on-a-mirror-instead-of-a-projector-screen/)
- **Ghosting:** standard back-silvered glass mirrors cause secondary reflections ("ghosting or haloing"); first-surface mirrors avoid this. — [AVS Forum, first-surface thread](https://www.avsforum.com/threads/front-projection-w-first-surface-mirror-question.601516/); [AVS Forum, bouncing off a mirror](https://www.avsforum.com/threads/bouncing-off-a-mirror.2094098/) (search summary did not say which thread)
- **Specular reflection and hotspots:**
  - Specular reflection returns light at a single angle, whereas diffuse reflection scatters it at many angles. — [US 10877189, mirror panel patent](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10877189)
  - The specular "hot-spot … glares the observer when it is directed to the viewer", so projector position and orientation must keep it away from viewers. — [US 12158578, vehicle glazing patent](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12158578)
- **PDLC as a screen:**
  - PDLC's opaque (no-voltage) state scatters light and can serve as a front-projection screen, ideally over a light absorber to limit washout. — [US 11768430, PDLC projection screen patent](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11768430)
  - One vendor engineers the opaque state for "high-contrast front or rear image projection", but notes its film is typically rear-projected and front projection is custom. — [Pro Display](https://prodisplay.com/smart-glass/switchable-smart-film/switchable-smart-film/); [Pro Display technologies](https://prodisplay.com/smart-glass/smart-glass-display-technologies/)

### Inferences
- **Where the light goes with clear holographic film on a mirror:**
  - Most of the projector's light passes through the film, hits the silver and leaves specularly.
  - If the projector sits above or below the mirror, that light lands on the floor, on the user's body or face (glare into the eyes), or on the opposite wall.
  - Only the film's small scattered fraction forms the visible image.
  - Viewers in line with the specular direction see a hotspot, and the glass adds a ghost double.
- **Washout, with illustrative numbers** (assumed values):
  - A perfect diffuse gain-1 screen of 0.5 m² lit by a 300-lumen pico projector reaches only about 190 cd/m² (L = Φ·G/πA).
  - 300 lux of room light on the same screen adds about 95 cd/m² of "black", giving roughly 3:1 contrast.
  - A transparent low-gain film on a mirror does worse.
  - So a pico projector is unsuitable in a lit room. Even a 4,000-lumen short-throw is marginal per the guide.
- **Placement:** the projector must sit in front of the mirror, on a shelf or the ceiling, where the user will shadow it, unless it is an ultra-short-throw unit mounted at the mirror's edge. This is not a "sticker" form factor.
- **A switchable variant is conceivable:**
  - PDLC film on the mirror, clear (powered) for normal mirror use and opaque (unpowered) as a projection screen for a camera-rendered try-on image.
  - This turns the overlay into a video see-through screen. It avoids the additive problem (Q7) but loses the reflection in that area.
  - No source found tests PDLC over a silver backing.

### Gaps
- No source tests holographic film on a silvered mirror, and no measured luminance or contrast for holographic film was found.
- Pico and short-throw projector prices and lumen specs were not researched in this pass.
- No clear-state transmittance, haze or drive-voltage figures were found for PDLC, and PDLC prices were not captured.

## 4. Other films: transparent LCD, e-paper, electrochromic, PDLC, two-way mirror film

### Takeaway
None of these films is a practical way to show the try-on image on a mirror:
- **Transparent LCD** needs light from behind, which a silvered mirror blocks.
- **E-paper** is opaque and takes seconds to refresh.
- **Electrochromic and PDLC** films can only dim or frost; they cannot form images.
- **Two-way mirror film** only works with a display behind clear glass.

PDLC (and, in principle, LCD) are the only layers that can subtract light. That makes them useful as dimming or occlusion helpers rather than as displays.

### Cited Findings
- **Transparent LCD:**
  - "An LCD does not emit light by itself… a non-transparent backlight unit is required at a rear surface." Light-guide plates and polarizers sharply reduce transmittance. These statements come from one of the transparent-display patents returned by search; the summary did not say which. — [US 9074749](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9074749); [US 9612477](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9612477); [US 10573230](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10573230)
  - A commercial guide describes transparent LCDs that drop the backlight and rely on ambient and object lighting instead. — [SLW](https://www.slw-ele.com/transparent-lcds-a-clear-advantage-for-industrial-displays.html)
- **Colour e-paper refresh is slow:**
  - Spectra 6 takes about 12 s per update. — [The eBook Reader](https://blog.the-ebook-reader.com/?p=25028); [invidis 2025](https://invidis.com/news/2025/03/e-ink-upgrade-more-colors-and-less-flashing/)
  - Spectra 6 Plus takes about 7 s. — [Good e-Reader](https://goodereader.com/blog/tag/e-ink-spectra-6-plus)
  - A 13.3" Spectra board needs 19 s for a full refresh and is "not designed for video". — [ThinkRobotics](https://thinkrobotics.com/es/products/13-3inch-e-ink-spectra)
  - A 7.3" E6 module takes about 25 s. — [RobotShop](https://uk.robotshop.com/products/73-inch-e-ink-spectra-6-e6-full-color-e-paper-display)
  - E Ink claims Gallery 3 updates "in as little as 500 ms" and that Kaleido 3 "can even play animations and videos" (vendor claims). — [E Ink blog](https://blog.eink.com/tech-thats-faster-brighter-and-more-colorful)
- **Electrochromic and PDLC:**
  - Both are used as "global dimming" or selectively occlusive layers in see-through AR. — [US 11789688](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11789688)
  - PDLC and SPD films lose electrical control above about 70 °C and can be permanently damaged above about 80 °C. — [US 10786975](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10786975)
  - Consumer PDLC window film is sold on Amazon; the listing notes it does not replace glass. — [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/Switchable-Privacy-Electric-Partitions-Projection/dp/B0G19FG27T)
- **Two-way mirror film:**
  - A DIY build applies mirrored window film to glass or acrylic and mounts a monitor *behind* it. — [Hackster](https://www.hackster.io/ben-eagan/smart-mirror-with-optional-alexa-874d43)
  - Bright pixels pass through while dark areas reflect, which is why these builds use white-on-black interfaces. — [Zbotic](https://zbotic.in/raspberry-pi-smart-mirror-build-a-magicmirror2-display-project/)
  - A builder reports that two-way mirror film "contains excessive metallic content and interferes with the touchscreen". Dielectric mirror glass is the higher-quality alternative. The search summary did not separate which build page says which. — [MagicMirror forum](https://forum.magicmirror.builders/topic/19472.rss); [Hackaday.io](https://www.hackaday.io/project/182911-magic-mirror)

### Inferences
- **Transparent LCD on a mirror: not viable.**
  - The silver blocks any backlight, so the panel could only work as a front-lit reflective LCD, like a calculator display.
  - The mirror sits 4–6 mm behind the cell, so the image would show parallax doubling, and polarizers plus colour filters would make it dim.
  - It is the only display type that could darken parts of the reflection (subtractive), but a rigid LCD panel is not a sticker and colour quality would be poor.
- **E-paper: not an overlay.**
  - It is opaque, so it cannot sit over a reflection.
  - A colour e-paper panel *beside* the mirror could show a still try-on render, updating in seconds, at low power.
  - That is a separate panel, not an overlay, and not live.
- **Electrochromic and PDLC: no images.**
  - PDLC is useful as a "mirror ↔ screen" switch for projection (Q3) or as a global dimmer.
  - Its heat sensitivity (about 70–80 °C) matters if it is stacked with LED film.
- **Two-way mirror film: adds nothing.**
  - It makes clear glass semi-reflective so that a display behind it shows through.
  - An existing silvered mirror has no "behind" to put a display in.
  - Its metal content can also break PCAP touch.

## 5. PCAP touch foils on a mirror

### Takeaway
All manufacturer and installer guidance found covers foils laminated *behind* glass, and it says the glass must be non-metallic. A foil behind a silvered mirror will not work.

No source addresses sticking a foil on the *front* of a silvered mirror. Physically, that resembles a touch sensor sitting over an LCD, which also has conductive layers behind the sensor. It may therefore work with controller tuning and a protective top layer, but it must be tested on a sample first.

## 7. The optics constraint: depth mismatch and additive-only light

### Takeaway
Research on smart mirrors and optical see-through AR confirms both problems:
1. **Depth mismatch.** Content on the mirror plane and the reflection (seen at twice the user's distance) cannot both be in focus, or fused by the two eyes, at the same time.
2. **Additive-only light.** A see-through emitter can only add light, so it cannot hide the clothes being worn. Dark garments render as "transparent".

No see-through overlay on an existing mirror avoids both. The approaches that do avoid them either need hardware behind the mirror, which a silvered household mirror cannot host, or replace the reflection with a camera image on an opaque screen. The second is what commercial try-on "mirrors" actually do.

### Cited Findings
- **3D-Mirrorcle:** smart mirrors with screens integrated into the reflective surface make it "difficult to see both the reflection and the 2D display at the same time" (depth disparity). The authors fix it with a lenticular grating and parallax/depth alignment. — [arXiv 2310.13617](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13617)
- **"Augment Yourself"** (optical see-through headset plus a physical mirror): when virtual content sits away from the reflection plane, users cannot focus on both at once. The alternative it discusses is a large display placed behind the mirror at a distance equal to the user's distance. That needs a large space and a fixed user distance. — [arXiv 2007.02884](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.02884)
- **MERL:** mirror beam-combiners can place the reflected image plane close to the physical object, reducing focal-plane differences. — [MERL TR2006-105](https://www.merl.com/publications/docs/TR2006-105.pdf)
- **Additive-only displays:**
  - Black is the absence of light, so a see-through display cannot emit it; where content should be black, the user sees the real scene. — [US 10001648](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10001648)
  - Without occlusion, virtual content looks "ghost-like". — [ResearchGate OST-HMD with occlusion](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/268194699_Optical_See-Through_Head-Mounted_Display_with_Occlusion_Capability)
  - Ambient light greatly degrades dark virtual images, and a liquid-crystal occlusion mask sits out of focus and blurs edges. — [J. Inf. Display 2021](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/15980316.2021.2018058)
  - Fixes need occlusive layers or global dimming (electrochromic or PDLC). — [US 11789688](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11789688)
  - Software colour correction is limited. — [US 9953556](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9953556)
- **Commercial try-on mirrors are video see-through:**
  - MemoMi's MemoryMirror is "not a mirror at all, but rather a 70-inch screen with a camera on top", relying on perspective-distortion correction. — [Reviewed](https://www.reviewed.com/laundry/news/this-smart-mirror-aims-to-digitize-the-fitting-room)
  - MemoMi is reportedly in more than 1,000 stores. — [RetailBoss](https://retailboss.co/memomi-smart-mirrors-now-in-over-1000-stores-as-ai-virtual-try-on-reduces-return-rates)
  - FXMirror merges an electronic mirror with an XL display and Kinect cameras for 3D fitting; its price is reported as S$45,000. — [Vulcan Post](https://vulcanpost.com/?p=550031)

### Inferences
My geometry, assuming an interpupillary distance (IPD) of about 63 mm and a user standing d metres from the mirror:

- **Scale.** The reflection appears at 2d, so on the glass it occupies exactly half life-size. A 0.5 × 1.0 m overlay therefore spans the reflection of a person up to about 2 m tall, and garments must be drawn at half scale.
- **Focus mismatch.** The overlay sits at d and the reflection at 2d, so the eye's focus difference is:

  | User distance d | Focus difference |
  |---|---|
  | 1.5 m | 0.33 D (borderline, near typical depth of focus) |
  | 1.0 m | 0.5 D |
  | 0.5 m | 1.0 D |

- **Two-eye (vergence) mismatch.** The difference is IPD/(2d): about 1.2° at 1.5 m and about 1.8° at 1.0 m. That is far beyond what the eyes can fuse. When the user looks at their reflection, the overlay garment appears doubled, and vice versa.
- **Registration error between the eyes.** The two eyes' lines of sight to any reflected body point cross the glass 31.5 mm apart (IPD/2), whatever the distance. An overlay drawn for one viewpoint is therefore off by ±16 mm on the glass for each eye. That is about ±3 cm at body scale, or about ±6 px at P2.5. Keeping it aligned also requires head tracking.
- **Additive light.** The overlay can only brighten.
  - It can paint a light garment over a dark one, because LED film at 1,000+ nits can outshine the reflection of clothes, which is only tens of cd/m² in normal room light (illustrative assumption).
  - It cannot paint a dark garment over light clothes, or remove the real garment's silhouette or sleeves.
  - Dark try-on items will look see-through.
- **Overlay approaches that avoid the constraint:**
  - Only an overlay region made opaque, showing a camera-captured, AI-rendered image of the user (video see-through), avoids it. That means an opaque panel, or a PDLC-frosted region with projection. Both give up the reflection in that region.
  - Overlays drawn at half scale and registered to one eye can at best act as coarse colour hints.
  - Lenticular/parallax fixes (3D-Mirrorcle) and behind-mirror displays need hardware behind a half-mirror, which an existing silvered mirror cannot host.

### Gaps
- The full text of the AR-mirror papers could not be opened, so no quantitative user-study numbers (such as discomfort ratings) were extracted.
- No source specifically covers virtual try-on overlays on a household mirror.

## 8. Electrical and fire safety of films on a surface people touch

### Takeaway
LED film runs on low-voltage DC: 5 V for Chinese film, and LG uses a 19.5 V DC adapter fed from 100–240 V AC. Shock risk at the film itself is therefore low if the PSU is certified. The real hazards are:
- high current at 5 V, tens of amps for 0.5 m² at full brightness;
- surface heat;
- unverified flame ratings for the PET substrate (one vendor claims "V1"; clear PET rated VTM-0 does exist);
- humidity in bathrooms.

Touch foils, IR frames and LiDAR run on USB or low-voltage DC and are low risk. No recalls or incidents specific to LED film were found.

### Cited Findings
- **LG LTAK140 and LAT140:**
  - Adapter: 100–240 V AC in, 19.5 V / 10.8 A (210 W) out.
  - Operating range 0–45 °C; "indoor glass installation only".
  - Sources: [LG UK LTAK140-GU](https://www.lg.com/uk/business/commercial-display/led-signage/indoor-led/ltak140-gu/); [LG LTAK140-GW](https://www.lg.com/global/business/led-signage/lg-ltak140-gw)
  - The LAT140 panel includes front and back protection film. — [LG LAT140](https://www.lg-informationdisplay.com/product/led-signage/transparent-led-film/LAT140)
- **Chinese film:** 5 V working voltage, ≤600 W/m² max. — [3CINNO](https://www.3cinno.com/media-mesh/transparent-led-film/)
- **DOIT VISION** (vendor claims):
  - CE, EMC and RoHS compliance.
  - A "UV-resistant and V1 flame-retardant rated" substrate.
  - A certification table listing UL, ETL, FCC, TÜV and CB, but the table is internally inconsistent.
  - Source: [DOIT VISION LED film](https://www.doitvision.com/product/led-film/)
- **UL 94 ratings:** V-0 means the sample self-extinguishes within 10 s with no flaming drips. Ratings depend on thickness: a material rated V-0 at 1.6 mm can be V-2 at 0.8 mm. The search summary did not say which page states which. — [Kabolux UL94 table](https://kabolux.com/en/ul94-flame-retardant-rating-comparison-table/); [Polycase](https://www.polycase.com/techtalk/ul-rated-plastic-enclosures/ul-94-flame-rating.html)
- **Clear flame-retardant PET with UL 94 VTM-0 exists:**
  - DuPont Teijin Melinex FR320/FR321, halogen-free. — [Mylar](https://mylar.com/news/2020/dupont-teijin-films-introduces-flame-retardant-clear-polyester-film/)
  - Tecfilm PET FI 13036. — [Müller-Ahlhorn](https://www.mueller-ahlhorn.com/en/tecfilm-pet-fi-13036-flame-retardant-transparent-polyester-film-dr-dietrich-mueller-gmbh/)
  - Ester EFR. — [Ester](https://esterindustries.com/flame-retardant-film-type-efr)
  - General-purpose clear PET, such as Toray's general Lumirror types, is only VTM-2. — [Toray](https://www.films.toray/en/ul/)
- **LED-specific standards:** a V-0 polycarbonate for LEDs was positioned as meeting UL 8750 guidelines (LED equipment for lighting). This is a separate standard from UL 94. — [LED Professional](https://www.led-professional.com/products/leds_led_modules/bayer-materialscience-develops-new-ul-94-v0-rated-flame-retardant-polycarbonate-plastic-for-leds)
- **LED temperature limit:** LED junction temperature is limited to about 125 °C; above that the encapsulant softens and LEDs fail (vendor blog). No authoritative surface-temperature data for LED film was found. — [Rayee](https://www.rayeeled.com/news/the-effect-of-transparent-led-film-screen-under-high-temperature-conditions-176914.html)
- **PDLC and SPD films:** they lose electrical control above about 70 °C and can be permanently damaged above about 80 °C. — [US 10786975](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10786975)

### Inferences
- **Shock.** At 5–20 V DC the film is extra-low-voltage; mains voltage stays inside the PSU or adapter.
  - Insist on a certified external PSU. For India this likely means BIS registration (not researched here).
  - Background, not sourced here: the EU Low Voltage Directive starts at 50 V AC / 75 V DC. The film itself therefore falls outside it, but the PSU does not.
- **Current and fire.** 0.5 m² at 200–600 W/m² draws 100–300 W, which is 20–60 A at 5 V.
  - Undersized connectors and ribbon tails are the most likely fire point.
  - Run the film dimmed for indoor use, and specify current-limited, fused PSUs.
- **Heat.** My rough estimate (Q1) suggests a rise of tens of °C at full vendor-rated power, but only a few °C when dimmed for indoor viewing. Get a thermal test at the intended brightness, especially where the user taps the surface.
- **Fire rating.** Ask for the UL file or test report for the actual substrate.
  - "V1" (DOIT VISION's claim) is weaker than VTM-0 or V-0, and plain PET can be only VTM-2.
  - A UL 94 rating alone is not an LED product safety certification.
- **Bathroom use.** LG restricts the film to indoor glass at 0–45 °C, and no humidity or IP rating was found. Bathrooms, with condensation and splashes, are therefore a risk for exposed LED film on a mirror.
- **Touch hardware.** PCAP foils (a passive film plus a USB controller), IR frames and LiDAR all run on USB or low-voltage DC, so their electrical risk is low.

### Gaps
- No recalls, CPSC notices or fire incidents specific to transparent LED film were found.
- No UL or IEC certification documents for the LG LAT140 were found.
- No humidity or IP ratings were found.
- Indian BIS requirements were not researched.
- No laser class was captured for LiDAR touch sensors.

## 9. Overall: which overlay approaches are viable for touch, for showing a try-on image, and which are not

### Takeaway
**Touch:** an existing mirror can be made interactive without replacing it.
- An IR frame is the best option.
- A LiDAR corner sensor also works.
- A PCAP foil mounted on the front of the mirror is the cheapest true "sticker", but it is untested on silvered mirrors.

**Showing the try-on image:** no film overlay delivers "see yourself wearing it" in the reflection.
- LED film is the only stick-on emitter, and it gives a low-resolution, additive, depth-mismatched ghost.
- The credible display path is an opaque screen showing a camera-rendered image (video see-through). An overlay can provide that only by covering part of the mirror.

### Cited Findings
- Commercial try-on "mirrors" are camera-plus-screen systems. MemoMi's is "not a mirror at all, but rather a 70-inch screen with a camera on top". — [Reviewed](https://www.reviewed.com/laundry/news/this-smart-mirror-aims-to-digitize-the-fitting-room)
- PCAP foils fail through metallised glass. — [WES Electronic](https://wes-electronic.de/en/news/blog/magic-mirror-touchscreen-behind-glass-or-mirror); [MagicMirror forum](https://forum.magicmirror.builders/topic/2538/touch-overly-vs-smart-glass)
- The finest LED film pitch found is P2.5 (vendor claims). — [SWX](https://swx-led.com/transparent-led-film/); [Muxwave](https://www.muxwave.com/product/series-m/)
- A projector aimed at a mirror throws the light back rather than forming an image. — [Linus Tech Tips forum](https://linustechtips.com/topic/1574151-projector-projecting-on-a-mirror-instead-of-a-projector-screen/)

### Inferences

**Viability matrix** (my synthesis of Q1–Q8; INR at the assumed ₹88/US$)

| Approach | Touch | Try-on image on the mirror | Notes / indicative cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| IR touch frame around the mirror | **Viable** (best) | — | 43" US$60–300 (≈ ₹5,000–26,000); needs a size match; raised bezel |
| LiDAR / laser-radar corner sensor | **Viable** (coarser) | — | About 290–1,775 (≈ ₹25,000–1.56 lakh); some Chinese listings omit the currency; needs calibration and careful mounting |
| PCAP nano foil on the FRONT of the mirror | **Conditional:** test first | — | 55" US$124–600 (≈ ₹11,000–53,000); needs a protective layer and controller tuning |
| PCAP foil BEHIND the mirror | Not viable | — | The silver blocks sensing |
| Transparent LED film (P2.5–P3.91) | — | **Limited:** coarse, ghostly and additive; fine for UI, icons and colour hints | About ₹67,000–1.35 lakh per 0.5 m² (IndiaMART list prices); halves the reflection or worse; visible grid; heat and current |
| LG LED film, P13.7 (LTAK/LAT) | — | Not viable for imagery | About 2,600 px over 0.5 m²; about US$2,779 per film |
| Transparent OLED | — | Not viable as an overlay | Rigid panels; US$4,000–21,500; 33–45% transparency |
| Holographic film + pico or short-throw projector | — | Not viable on a mirror | Specular hotspot, ghosting, washout |
| PDLC (opaque state) + projector | — | **Conditional:** only as an opaque "screen mode" (video see-through) | Loses the reflection in that zone; projector placement is awkward |
| Transparent LCD | — | Not viable | Needs a backlight, which the silver blocks |
| E-paper | — | Not as an overlay (opaque; 0.5–25 s refresh) | Could be a side panel for still images |
| Electrochromic film / two-way mirror film | — | Not viable | No image / needs a display behind clear glass |

- **The product-defining constraint is Q7.** Even a perfect transparent overlay would sit at the wrong depth, be misregistered between the eyes, and be unable to hide the clothes being worn.
- **A realistic "sticker kit" splits the jobs.** Touch hardware goes on the mirror (an IR frame or a foil). The try-on render goes on a camera plus an opaque display: a phone, tablet or TV, or an opaque panel or PDLC region covering part of the mirror.
- **Transparent LED film is most defensible as a menu/UI layer**, such as large icons, text and colour swatches, not as the try-on image.

### Gaps
- Every viability call on mirror-specific behaviour rests on physical reasoning, not tests. This covers:
  - a front-mounted PCAP foil;
  - how LED film looks on a silvered surface;
  - LiDAR near specular glass;
  - PDLC over silver.
- A bench test with a sample foil and a sample of LED film on a household mirror would settle these quickly.
