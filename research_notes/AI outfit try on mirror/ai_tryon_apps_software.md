## 1. Digital wardrobe / AI stylist apps: how clothes get in, auto-tagging, outfit suggestions, on-body view, platform, pricing, scale, status

### Takeaway
Digitising your own wardrobe on a phone is a crowded, mature category. The leaders are Whering (10M users, US$7M seed in July 2026) and Acloset (8–9M users claimed). Alta (US$11M seed, TIME Best Invention 2025) is the one funded app that already shows the user's OWN clothes on a photo-real avatar of the user. Most other apps still show flat-lay cut-outs, not the person. Prices are low: free with affiliate income, about US$3–5 one-off, or about US$60–75 a year. Archivist services (Indyx, US$295 per 100 items) show that getting clothes into the app is still the main friction.

### Inferences
- Getting clothes into the app is the main friction across the category. Evidence: receipt forwarding (Alta, Pronti), photo auto-tagging (Acloset, GetWardrobe), Whering's planned gallery scanning, and a paid human archivist (Indyx, US$295). A mirror with a camera that **passively logs what the user actually wears each day** would attack this friction directly. This is a design inference, not a sourced fact.
- Alta is the closest existing software to the mirror idea: your own wardrobe on a lifelike version of you. But it is a phone avatar render, not a live camera view. Whering, with Google AI Futures Fund backing, has said it plans to add try-on. So "see yourself in your own clothes" is likely to become a common phone feature by 2027.
- Approximate launch years from Apple App Store ID ranges (IDs rise over time; **approximate inference only**): Stylebook (id335709058) ≈ 2009; Smart Closet (id1198057728) ≈ early 2017; Acloset (id1542311809) ≈ late 2020; Indyx (id1599179405) ≈ late 2021; Alta (id6481705400) ≈ 2024.
- Indicative INR equivalents at an **assumed ~₹88/US$ (not sourced)**: Acloset Premium US$59.99/yr ≈ ₹5,300; Indyx Insider US$74.99/yr ≈ ₹6,600; Stylebook US$4.99 ≈ ₹440. Apple and Google set local Indian price tiers, so actual store prices in rupees may differ.
- Consumers expect wardrobe apps to be free or cheap. A mirror cannot charge for "digital closet + outfit suggestions" alone; it must charge for the hardware experience.

## 2. AI try-on on the user's own body: Google, Doppl, Doji, Alta, Glance AI, Perfect Corp, Zalando/Zara, Amazon, Walmart, Indian e-commerce, Snapchat, Instagram/TikTok

### Takeaway
Single-photo generative try-on is now free and mass-market. Google Search's "try it on" has run in the US since July 2025 and in India and the UK since 3 December 2025. Google shut its standalone video try-on app **Doppl on 30 April 2026** and folded try-on into Search. Retailers (Zara, Zalando), lock-screen and TV platforms (Glance AI on Samsung phones and TVs) and avatar apps (Alta, Doji) all generate images or short clips of the user. Indian marketplaces are still at pilot stage: Myntra is piloting try-on from customer photos (July 2026), and Flipkart lists "virtual try-ons" only as an investment area. None of these show the user's own wardrobe live on camera.

## 3. Licensable SDKs and APIs: real-time AR clothing try-on vs generative (photo) try-on

### Takeaway
The licensable market splits in two:
- **Live-camera AR SDKs** (Snap Camera Kit, Kivisense, DeepAR, Banuba, WANNA). These run in real time but need a 3D asset per garment. Most published pricing is for face and accessory categories, not clothing.
- **Generative photo APIs** (Google Vertex AI Virtual Try-On ≈ US$0.06/image, FASHN ≈ US$0.075/image in 5–17 s, Perfect Corp ≈ US$0.10–0.11/result). These work from a single garment photo, which suits a user's own clothes, but are not real-time.

B2B fashion players (3DLOOK, Vue.ai, Veesual, Revery.ai) quote prices on request and target retailers.

### Inferences
- For a mirror that must show the user's **own** clothes, the generative photo APIs are the practical building block today. They need only one photo of each garment, which a wardrobe-digitising flow already captures. Live-AR SDKs need a per-garment 3D asset, which is impractical for an ordinary person's closet unless the mirror can auto-generate 3D garments.
- **Rough cost model (inference with assumed usage):** 5 generated looks per user per day × 30 days = 150 images a month. That costs about US$9 on Vertex (US$0.06), about US$11 on FASHN (US$0.075), or about US$14–17 on Perfect Corp. That is roughly ₹800–1,450 a month per user at an assumed ₹88/US$. Cloud generation costs could exceed what Indian consumers pay for wardrobe apps, which points to caching, on-device models, or a subscription.
- DeepAR's own warning that MAU pricing is unstable for photo booths applies directly to a **shared household mirror**. Licensing should be negotiated per device rather than per MAU.
- Snap's 2026 opening of the Clothing Try-On Lens to Camera Kit lowers the barrier to live AR for **catalogue** items, but not for the user's own items.

- Not checked: Kling AI's try-on API, Amazon Nova Canvas try-on, SpreeAI, DRESSX's B2B SDK. DRESSX markets an "AI Stylist with Virtual Try-On" at [dressxagent.com](https://dressxagent.com/) (vendor claim).

## 4. Accuracy and speed in practice; does anything do real-time live video with the user's own clothes?

### Takeaway
Reviewers call generative try-on "fun and impressive but inconsistent". Fabric drape and realism impress. Fit, layering, rise and length, and accessories go wrong. Speed is seconds per image via API and about 2–5 minutes per avatar or outfit in retail apps (Zara: about 2 minutes per look, 5 looks a day). **No product was found that does real-time live video try-on of a user's own digitised wardrobe.** Live AR exists only for 3D-modelled catalogue items, and live generative try-on exists only in vendor marketing and research.

### Cited Findings
- **Doppl**:
  - The Verge's hands-on, as quoted in coverage: "I just tried on five different outfits in about 10 minutes", but "it has clear issues putting pants on photos of me wearing shorts, and it even replaced my mismatched socks with AI-generated feet in one instance" (original Verge URL not retrieved) — [ETCentric roundup](https://www.etcentric.org/google-doppl-lets-you-try-on-outfits-using-generative-video/)
  - Other reviewers called it early-stage with features missing — [re:dressing](https://redressing.substack.com/p/doppl-review) — and "far from flawless" (Perfect Corp, a competitor) — [Perfect Corp](https://www.perfectcorp.com/consumer/blog/ai-clothes/google-launches-doppl-for-virtual-try-on)
  - An unofficial review site reports occasional clipping, only tops, bottoms and dresses (no bags, shoes or jewellery), and monthly generation limits (unverified) — [doppl.world](https://www.doppl.world/blog/doppl-ai-review-2025)
- **Google Search try-on**: Google warns that output may contain errors in body shape, personal features or clothing details, and that quality depends on the merchant's images — [Google Merchant Center Help](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/16159685?hl=en). A secondary review calls it "impressively realistic, but not 100% perfect" — [SEO Sherpa](https://seosherpa.com/google-virtual-try-on/)
- **Zara**: about 2 minutes per outfit, capped at 5 looks a day — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-21/zalando-zara-use-ai-virtual-try-ons-to-tackle-clothing-returns). Avatar wait of 2–5 minutes; "slightly glitchy" — [NY Post via AOL](https://www.aol.com/news/groundbreaking-ai-tool-dress-few-120000041.html). One reviewer went from sceptical to impressed — [Woman & Home](https://www.womanandhome.com/fashion/fashion-news/i-was-sceptical-about-zaras-new-virtual-try-on-tool-but-it-really-impressed-me-its-fun-and-takes-all-the-guesswork-out-of-online-shopping/). Stellar ran an "is it actually any good?" test — [Stellar](https://stellar.ie/beauty-fashion/is-the-new-zara-try-on-tool-actually-any-good/147042)
- **Zalando**: Bloomberg's tester found the avatars uncomfortable to use and the jeans not convincingly fitted. Virtual try-on cannot reproduce how clothes *feel*, and no platform has published a return-rate reduction attributable solely to try-on — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-21/zalando-zara-use-ai-virtual-try-ons-to-tackle-clothing-returns)
- **Alta**: App Store reviews range from "weirdly very accurate" to complaints that mid- and low-rise jeans render as high-rise — [App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/alta-daily-digital-ai-closet/id6481705400?see-all=reviews&platform=iphone). A 2026 guide notes uncanny-valley face artefacts, slow navigation between saved looks, weak hot-weather styling, and a cap of 8 pieces per avatar look (competitor-authored) — [PutTogether guide](https://www.puttogether.world/guides/digital-closet/ai-outfit-app-that-draws-you-2026)
- **Doji**: at its 2025 launch it did not simulate fit or fabric behaviour — [UrbanGeekz](https://urbangeekz.com/2025/05/doji-14m-ai-fashion-avatars/)
- **Glance AI**: multiple looks "in under three minutes" (store listing) — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.glance.ailockscreen&hl=en_GB)
- **API speed**: FASHN takes 5 s (Performance) to 12–17 s (Quality) per image — [FASHN docs](https://docs.fashn.ai/api-reference/tryon-v1-6)
- **Live or real-time**:
  - Kivisense's consumer AI Clothes Changer offers a 30-second "real-time live try-on"; the vendor claims switching has no lag (not independently verified) — [Kivisense](https://tryon.kivisense.com/ai-clothes-changer/)
  - Kivisense's AR clothing uses 3D garments with body tracking and cloth simulation; weak phones struggle — [Kivisense docs](https://tryon-docs.kivisense.com/ar-clothing-try-on)
  - Snap's Clothing Try-On Lens is live AR for prepared catalogue lenses — [Snap AR blog](https://ar.snap.com/blog/snap-camera-kit-ar-sdk)
  - A 2026 vendor blog claims generative try-on now renders "in real time, using their own camera" (promotional, no benchmark) — [Fynd blog](https://www.fynd.com/blog/how-generative-ai-is-transforming-virtual-try-ons-in-e-commerce-in-2026)
  - Research is moving toward video try-on and real-time interactive video generation — [TryOnCrafter (arXiv 2606.26092)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.26092); [Vidu S2 real-time interactive video generation (arXiv 2609.11638)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.11638); [garment-fidelity evaluation (arXiv 2608.29804)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.29804)
  - Real-time generative camera tools exist for faces and avatars (xpression camera; Akool Live Camera, May 2025), not clothing — [xpression camera](https://xpressioncamera.com/); [Akool (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akool)

### Inferences
- Several reported errors come from **the input photo**: shorts in the source photo broke pants try-on, and Zara needs both a selfie and a full-length shot. A mirror that captures a standardised, well-lit, full-length, multi-angle image every day could improve accuracy over casual phone selfies. This is a plausible technical advantage, but untested.
- At today's 5–17 s per generated image, a mirror can feel "near-live" only with tricks: pre-generating likely outfits overnight, showing a live AR silhouette while the photoreal render loads, and caching per pose. True live photoreal video of arbitrary own garments is at research stage, not a product.
- Accuracy complaints centre on **fit and length**: rise, pants length, socks and feet. Generative try-on renders appearance, not true fit. For the user's own clothes, fit is already known (they own them), so a mirror's job is mostly styling visualisation. That is a lower accuracy bar than shopping try-on and a favourable point for the concept.

### Gaps
- No independent quantitative benchmark (fidelity scores, latency, user error rates) comparing Google, Zara, Alta, Glance and Doji was found.
- No primary Verge, Wired or WSJ article was opened. The Verge quote comes via a roundup.
- No evidence was found of any product doing live video try-on with garments **the user photographed themselves**. Kivisense's live mode input type (3D asset vs uploaded photo) is unclear.

## 5. Influencer and inspiration looks: importing a look from Instagram, Pinterest or an influencer and recreating it from your own closet

### Takeaway
A few apps already let users upload a Pinterest or social screenshot and match it to items they own; Wardrobe.AI is the most explicit. Shopping-side tools let users try a celebrity or influencer look on themselves (Doppl did this before it shut; Doji accepts outside product links). No major app was found that combines both: "see the influencer look recreated from MY closet, rendered on ME".

### Cited Findings
- **Wardrobe.AI**: upload a photo from social media or Pinterest and the AI matches the style to items in the digital closet. It can also build outfits around one chosen item, with explanations — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.chicai.closet)
- **AIStyle (iOS)**: outfit changing from the user's closet, plus looks from users, stylists and influencers — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6753858331)
- **Nouva**: complete-the-look from the user's closet first, recommending purchases only for gaps — [nouva.app](https://www.nouva.app/)
- **Pronti**: a chat stylist that draws on the user's closet, saved outfits and style history — [pronti.com](https://www.pronti.com/)
- **Doppl**: a reviewer "tried on Cardi B's Met Gala outfit" from a photo, showing celebrity-look try-on on the user's own body — [re:dressing](https://redressing.substack.com/p/doppl-review); positioned as "try on any outfit" — [iamdave.ai](https://www.iamdave.ai/blog/doppl-app-by-google-how-to-virtually-try-on-any-outfit/). (App shut down 30 April 2026 — [Google Labs Help](https://support.google.com/labs/answer/16537062?hl=en))
- **Doji**: users can upload links to outside apparel to see an item on their avatar — [POCIT](https://peopleofcolorintech.com/media/fashion/fashion-tech-startup-raises-14m-to-improve-virtual-try-on-app/)
- **Google**: after Doppl, try-on works on "product listings and image results across Google" — [Google Labs on X](https://x.com/GoogleLabs/status/2036188820128071786)
- **Whering**: plans "gallery scanning" to identify clothing in users' own photos — [Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2026/07/07/whering-lands-7m-as-digital-wardrobe-platform-reaches-10m-users/)
- **Veesual** (B2B) sells "Look Inspiration" and "Mix&Match" modules to retailers — [Caplight](https://www.caplight.com/company/veesual)
- **Ajio** reportedly runs "Ajio Studio" virtual styling sessions with influencers (unverified blog claim) — [Synarion blog](https://blog.synarionit.com/top-fashion-shopping-apps-in-india/)
- **Glance AI** positions itself as "aspirational" AI-generated looks of the user — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/glance-ai-looks-to-usher-in-aspirational-ecommerce-will-ai-shopping-take-off/)
- **DIY precedent**: a developer built a stylist that pulls fashion influencers' Instagram feeds and matches them to owned clothes using Google's Vision Product Search API (about five years old) — [Dale on AI](https://daleonai.com/social-media-fashion-ai)

### Inferences
- The building blocks exist separately: look-to-closet matching (Wardrobe.AI), own-wardrobe avatar rendering (Alta), and generative try-on of arbitrary images (Google, Vertex AI). A mirror that chains them, "pick a Pinterest or Instagram look → closest matches from your closet → render on you in the mirror", would be a distinct experience. It is also achievable as a phone feature, so it is not defensible by itself.
- Social platforms (Snap, TikTok, Instagram) do not offer "recreate from my closet", because their incentive is to sell new items. This leaves the own-closet angle to independent apps and devices.

### Gaps
- Whether Alta, Whering, Acloset or Indyx support importing a screenshot or Pinterest pin and matching it to owned items was not confirmed.
- No Indian app with influencer-look-to-own-closet matching was found.
- Pinterest's own features (e.g., "shuffles" or try-on) were not checked.

## 6. What gap remains for a home touchscreen smart mirror, and where phone apps already do the job well enough

### Takeaway
Phones already do wardrobe cataloguing, weather- and occasion-aware outfit suggestions, and single-photo try-on of shop items well enough, for free or nearly free. The open space is a **hands-free, full-body, live view of the user wearing combinations of their own clothes, captured and updated passively at the moment of dressing**. No software product found does this. The nearest are Alta (own-closet avatar on a phone), Glance AI on Samsung TVs (big screen, catalogue looks, no camera), and live AR lenses (catalogue 3D assets only).

### Cited Findings (evidence for what phones already cover)
- **Scale of phone wardrobe apps**: Whering has 10M users — [Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2026/07/07/whering-lands-7m-as-digital-wardrobe-platform-reaches-10m-users/). Acloset claims 8–9M — [Wanted.co.kr](https://www.wanted.co.kr/wd/391802). GetWardrobe says 3M — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.ThreeBoots&hl=en_IN)
- **Outfit suggestions by weather and occasion** already exist: Acloset — [App Store KR](https://apps.apple.com/KR/app/id1542311809); Alta (weather, occasion, budget, calendar) — [altadaily.com](https://www.altadaily.com/)
- **Own-closet-on-you rendering** already exists on phones: Alta's avatar — [PutTogether guide](https://www.puttogether.world/guides/digital-closet/ai-outfit-app-that-draws-you-2026); Whering's try-on is planned — [Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2026/07/07/whering-lands-7m-as-digital-wardrobe-platform-reaches-10m-users/)
- **Free shop try-on in India**: Google Search try-on since 3 December 2025 — [Google India blog](https://blog.google/intl/en-in/products/virtual-apparel-try-on-tool-comes-to-india/)
- **Big-screen home try-on is being tried in software**: Glance AI on Samsung TVs (June 2026), where virtual wardrobes on TV need no phone — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260623768093/en/Glance-and-Samsung-Unveil-Agentic-Commerce-Experience-on-Millions-of-U.S.-Smart-TVs); [Prosper Show](https://prospershow.com/news/from-tv-screen-to-ai-powered-shopping-experience/151254/)
- **Standalone try-on apps struggle**: Doppl shut after about 10 months — [Google Labs Help](https://support.google.com/labs/answer/16537062?hl=en). Alle (India) reportedly shut after six pivots (secondary) — [Medium](https://medium.com/illumination/alle-apps-3m-ai-fashion-stylist-dream-died-in-six-pivots-97a79c85a359). Snap closed its enterprise try-on unit in 2023 — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/09/27/snap-shutters-its-enterprise-services-division-after-less-than-a-year/embed/)
- **Current limits a mirror could target**:
  - Zara: about 2 minutes per look, 5 looks a day — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-21/zalando-zara-use-ai-virtual-try-ons-to-tackle-clothing-returns)
  - Doppl's errors depended on the source photo (shorts vs pants) — [ETCentric](https://www.etcentric.org/google-doppl-lets-you-try-on-outfits-using-generative-video/)
  - Google needs a full-length photo — [Google blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/shopping/google-shopping-ai-mode-virtual-try-on-update/)
  - Alta caps 8 pieces per avatar look — [PutTogether guide](https://www.puttogether.world/guides/digital-closet/ai-outfit-app-that-draws-you-2026)
  - Getting clothes into the app is costly: Indyx charges US$295 per 100 items — [myindyx.com](https://www.myindyx.com/how-it-works)
- **Privacy**: Zalando reportedly hides faces on its avatars — [clad.you](https://clad.you/blog/posts/virtual-tryon-comparison/). Glance's opt-in lock-screen approach has drawn "pushy" criticism — [AIM](https://analyticsindiamag.com/ai-features/glance-is-so-pushy)

### Inferences
**Where a mirror could be unique** (design inferences, untested):
1. **Hands-free, full-body capture at the moment of dressing.** It solves the input-quality problem that hurts phone try-on, which depends on a good full-length photo.
2. **Passive wardrobe digitising and wear logging.** The camera sees what the user actually puts on, turning the category's biggest friction (manual cataloguing, paid archivists) into a by-product of use. Whering is only now planning gallery scanning to do this from phone photos.
3. **Live pose with photoreal overlay of OWN garments.** This is not available anywhere. It would likely need a hybrid: live AR body tracking plus pre- or near-real-time generative renders (5–17 s per image today), or 3D garment reconstruction from the mirror's own scans.
4. **Shared household device.** Multiple family members with one display. Licensing must avoid per-MAU pricing (see DeepAR's photo-booth caveat).
5. **Indian context.** Google's try-on reportedly excludes sarees and lehengas (vendor claim). Indian marketplaces are at pilot stage. Draped and ethnic garments are a likely technical and market gap, but validation is needed.

**Where phones are "good enough"**: outfit planning and calendars, weather and occasion suggestions, resale and cost-per-wear tracking, quick try-on of a shop item before buying (free in Google Search), and inspiration feeds. A mirror should treat a phone app as companion and catalogue, not compete with it.

**Strategic risks:**
- Google, Samsung/Glance and Zara are subsidising try-on as a shopping funnel, so its perceived value is near zero.
- Generative inference costs per look (≈US$0.06–0.11) add up for a daily-use device.
- Alta or Whering could add a camera-based "mirror mode" on phones or tablets on a stand, closing much of the gap without new hardware.

### Gaps
- No consumer research was found on whether people want a live mirror view versus a phone render, or on willingness to pay in India.
- No data was found on how often wardrobe-app users actually open the app daily (retention), which would test the "daily ritual at the mirror" thesis.
- Hardware smart-mirror competitors are deliberately out of scope (another researcher covers them). Any software they bundle, such as mirror-maker companion apps, was not checked.
- The search budget ran out, so several apps (Combyne, OpenWardrobe, Fits, Save Your Wardrobe status) and platform features (Instagram/TikTok AR try-on) remain unverified.
