## 1. Retail and in-store products that combined a mirror (or a portrait "digital mirror" screen), a camera and virtual clothing try-on

### Takeaway
More than a dozen retail systems have offered some form of mirror-plus-camera clothing try-on since 2011. They came in three waves:
1. **2011–2015: depth-camera AR mirrors.** These used Kinect or Intel RealSense cameras to overlay the retailer's 2D or 3D garments on the shopper in real time. Examples: Cisco StyleMe, FaceCake Swivel, Uniqlo Magic Mirror, Fitnect, Samsung's mirror OLED and FXMirror.
2. **2014–2018: "smart fitting-room" mirrors.** These mostly recorded video, read RFID tags and collected analytics rather than drawing garments on the shopper. Examples: MemoMi, Oak Labs and Alibaba FashionAI.
3. **2022–2026: AR and generative-AI mirrors.** Examples: Snap AR Mirrors, the DRESSX Mirror, INDE's Vyu AI and LOOOK.AI, which uses real-time video AI.

**Every one of them renders the retailer's catalogue, not the shopper's own clothes.** Most early pilots lasted days or weeks. Nothing from the first two waves survived as a standalone business.

#### Wave 1: depth-camera AR fitting mirrors (2011–2015)
- **Cisco StyleMe "virtual fashion mirror"** (Cisco IBSG with John Lewis, UK; piloted 2011–2012). A life-sized mirror that overlaid pictures of clothing on the shopper's image. Shoppers picked garments by gesture or touch. Built-in 3D cameras captured the shopper's dimensions and position. — [Design Week, Dec 2011](https://www.designweek.co.uk/issues/december-2011/the-team-and-cisco-develop-virtual-mirrors-for-john-lewis/); [Cisco StyleMe overview (PDF)](https://www3-realm.cisco.com/web/about/ac79/docs/retail/StyleMeEngagementOverview_120611FINAL.pdf)

Shoppers could email images or share them on social networks. — [Computer Weekly](https://www.computerweekly.com/news/2240148889/John-Lewis-pilots-virtual-fashion-mirrors); [The Retail Bulletin, 26 Apr 2012](https://www.theretailbulletin.com/news/john_lewis_trials_virtual_fashion_mirrors_26-04-12/)
  - Pilot results: 1,402 shoppers used it (more than 40 a day) and 67% rated it positively. It was built with C-instore, Aitech and The Team. — [Cisco case study (PDF)](https://www3-realm.cisco.com/web/about/ac79/docs/retail/John-Lewis-and-Cisco-Virtual-fashion-mirror-case-study.pdf). The shopper chose a look with a wave of the hand and saw it overlaid on her image on an HD flat screen in real time. Snapshots could be shared online. No evidence was found that it continued after the 2012 test.

### Inferences
- **No retail system ingests the shopper's own wardrobe.** The closest exceptions: the Uniqlo mirror only recoloured a garment the shopper was physically wearing, and MemoMi recorded real try-ons.
- **Most systems are "digital mirrors", not true mirrors.** They show a camera feed on a screen (Swivel, Snap, DRESSX, Vyu, LOOOK.AI). Overlaying garments on an optical reflection was tried mainly by Uniqlo (half-mirror) and Samsung (mirror OLED).
  - Amazon's patent (see §6) shows why: lining up virtual clothes with a real reflection requires tracking where the viewer's face and eyes are.
- **Wave 1 needed per-item garment assets and depth cameras.** It was expensive (S$45k per FXMirror unit) and stayed at pilot stage.
- **Wave 2 shifted the selling point to analytics, RFID and recording.** That suggests retailers valued operational data more than rendered try-on.
- **Wave 3 removes the 3D-modelling cost by generating garments from product photos.** Real-time, movement-following video try-on only became commercially available in 2026, with Decart Lucy 2.0 powering LOOOK.AI. This is the key enabling technology for the product idea.

## 2. Home and consumer mirror-type devices, CES 2024–2026 showings, and 2025–2026 launches

**No home device has ever shipped virtual clothing try-on.** The two big home products closest to this idea were both discontinued:

## 3. Why earlier attempts failed or were discontinued

### Takeaway
The sourced reasons fall into five groups:
1. **The operator folded the feature into an app.** Echo Look's features moved into the Amazon Shopping app.
2. **The economics of home hardware collapsed.** MIRROR took a US$443M impairment and its hardware was discontinued.
3. **AR mirror programmes were too complex and costly, and generative AI overtook them.** Snap shut down ARES.
4. **Startups were bought and pivoted.** Oak Labs became ZIVELO's OakOS kiosk software; Memomi's technology ended up in Walmart's eyewear try-on.
5. **Most clothing-mirror pilots lasted days or weeks with no recorded rollout.** Swivel ran 10 days, FashionAI 3 days, and StyleMe stayed a trial.

### Cited Findings
- **Snap:** Evan Spiegel's memo said web-based AR needed ongoing investment, was technically complex and was "less engaging for our customers". Generative AI had made it easier for brands to build their own try-on, and weak revenue left less money for the venture. About 170 jobs were cut. — [Retail TouchPoints](https://www.retailtouchpoints.com/news/citing-rise-of-generative-ai-snap-nixes-ares-white-label-ar-offering/137870/); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/snaps-shutting-down-enterprise-ar-development-project/695010/); [The Daily Upside](https://www.thedailyupside.com/technology/social-media/snap-squashes-its-nascent-ar-enterprise-division/)
- **Amazon:** its stated reason for ending Echo Look was that much of the device's functionality, including Style by Alexa, was now in the Amazon Shopping app. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/05/29/echo-looks-will-cease-functioning-in-july-as-amazon-discontinues-the-camera)
- **Lululemon:** a US$443M Mirror impairment (March 2023), a price cut from ~US$1,500 to US$995, hardware discontinued at the end of 2023, workout content handed to Peloton, and about 120 layoffs. — [RetailWire](https://retailwire.com/discussion/what-does-lululemon-see-when-it-looks-in-its-mirror/); [Retail Dive](https://www.retaildive.com/news/lululemon-discontinues-mirror-peloton-apparel-fitness-content-partnership/694995/)
- **Oak Labs:** acquired in stock by ZIVELO, whose stated aim was to add a developer kit (OakOS) to its kiosk and digital-signage hardware. — [Kiosk Marketplace](https://www.kioskmarketplace.com/news/zivelos-acquisition-of-oak-labs-designed-to-simplify-kiosk-digital-sign-deployment/); [AV Network](https://www.avnetwork.com/news/zivelo-acquires-oak-labs)
- **Memomi:** before Walmart bought it in 2022, its large-scale use was eyewear try-on in Walmart Vision Centers (2,800+) and Sam's Clubs (550), not clothing. — [NoCamels](https://nocamels.com/?p=113032); [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/memomi-labs)
- **Pilot lengths:** Swivel ran at Bloomingdale's from 6–16 September 2012. — [Sixteen:Nine](https://www.sixteen-nine.net/?p=4907). - **Kinect-era limit:** outside of a Kinect, homes had no depth camera. — [Zugara](https://zugara.com/augmented-reality-3d-virtual-fitting-rooms-the-good-the-bad)
- **Unit cost:** about S$45,000 for one FXMirror at a Singapore bridal shop. — [Vulcan Post](https://vulcanpost.com/?p=550031). CareOS Artemis was reportedly US$20,000. — [Next Reality](https://next.reality.news/news/artemis-smart-mirror-shows-off-ar-vanity-for-cotys-wella-professionals-salons-0192340)
- **Cost of real-time generative try-on:** Decart Lucy 2.0 is "currently too expensive for retail" at US$0.05 per second. — [Genlook](https://genlook.app/blog/decart-ai-lucy-2-realtime-virtual-try-on)
- **Demand claim (unverified):** DRESSX cites a 2023 Shopify study saying fitting-room hesitation causes more than 30% of in-store browse-and-leave behaviour. This is vendor marketing and the study couldn't be verified. — [DRESSX](https://dressx.com/news/how-dressx-mirror-is-transforming-virtual-try-on-in-physical-retail)

### Inferences
- **Content cost was probably the structural problem for 2011–2019 try-on mirrors.** Every item had to be photographed or 3D-modelled and rigged (FXGear's physics simulation, Snap's AR assets). Retailers with seasonal catalogues are unlikely to keep that up. Generating garments from product photos (LOOOK.AI, Vyu, DRESSX) is aimed squarely at this problem.
- **Overlay realism was likely poor.** FXGear's 2018 switch from overlaying clothes on the shopper's image to an avatar points to a realism problem. No user study was found to confirm this.
- **Single-purpose home devices get absorbed into phone apps.** Echo Look and MIRROR show that if a phone app can deliver the value, the device gets folded into the app. A home try-on mirror has to offer something a phone can't, such as a full-length live view without holding anything.
- **Cloud inference is too expensive for home use at today's prices.** At US$0.05 per second, a 5-minute live try-on costs about US$15, or about US$180 per hour of continuous use. A home subscription can't absorb that, so on-device or much cheaper models would be needed. (Calculated from Genlook's figure.)

### Gaps
- No first-hand statements were found on why Swivel, StyleMe, the Uniqlo Magic Mirror, Fitnect or FXMirror didn't scale.
- Apart from StyleMe's usage figures, no pilot reported conversion or return-rate data.

## 6. Key patents on AR and virtual try-on mirrors (Amazon, Snap, others)

### Takeaway
**The anchor patent is Amazon's US 9,858,719 B2, granted 2 January 2018.** It describes a "blended reality" mirror: partially reflective glass with a display behind it, plus projectors and cameras, that shows the user in virtual clothes. It was never turned into a product, and Google Patents lists it as expired for non-payment of fees (an automated status, not a legal finding).

More recent IP that is still active:
- **Smart mirrors that generate clothing recommendations:** ACI Holdings' US 11,602,232, continued as US 12,426,728.
- **Real-time AR garment try-on on live video:** Snap's 2024–2025 filings.

No Samsung or Meta patents on try-on mirrors were found in the time available.

- **Two groups of patents matter most for freedom to operate** on "recommend from the wardrobe, then render on live video":
  - the ACI Holdings family (recommendations on a smart mirror; 2019 priority; active);
  - Snap's real-time try-on filings.

## 7. Does any product do exactly this: home use + touch mirror + the user's own wardrobe + real-time, motion-following try-on? If not, which is closest and what is missing?

### Takeaway
**No.** Nothing found as of October 2026 combines all four. The pieces exist separately:

| Product | What it has | What it lacks |
|---|---|---|
| **HEYMIRROR** (EONEOMS, CES 2026) | Home use; touch and voice; the user's own wardrobe; AI-generated outfit previews | Any evidence of live, motion-following try-on |
| **LOOOK.AI** (March 2026) | Real-time, movement-following generative try-on built from product photos | Home use and the user's own wardrobe (it is retail, uses the catalogue, and is controlled by phone) |
| **FXMirror True Fit** (2018) | Real-time avatar that copies the user's movements | Home use and own clothes (retail, 3D catalogue garments) |

### Inferences
- **HEYMIRROR is the closest overall:** same use case, same home setting. Compared with the idea, it is missing:
  1. live, camera-based try-on that follows the user's movements and draws a wardrobe item on their mirror image;
  2. any evidence that it has a camera;
  3. a confirmed consumer price and availability outside Korea;
  4. any presence in India.
- **LOOOK.AI with Decart is the closest technically.** It is missing:
  1. a home form factor and price;
  2. a way to take in the user's own garments, though its "product photo to try-on" pipeline suggests photos of a user's own clothes could work in principle;
  3. a touch-on-glass interface;
  4. affordable inference costs.
- **The idea is not already a product, but its novelty is narrow.** What's new is the combination: bringing 2026-era real-time video try-on into a home mirror that takes in the user's own clothes. Expect fast followers such as EONEOMS, LOOOK.AI/FFFACE.ME, or Qualcomm reference designs.
- **Patents to check before building:**
  - Amazon US 9,858,719: if it really has lapsed, as Google Patents suggests, that lowers the risk for an optical-mirror design.
  - ACI Holdings US 11,602,232 / US 12,426,728 (active).
  - Snap's 2024–2025 real-time try-on filings.

### Gaps
- HEYMIRROR's hardware (whether it has a camera, whether the display sits under the glass) and whether its AI-generated content shows the user's own body could not be checked.
- Indiegogo and Chinese home "AI fitting mirror" products could not be searched before the budget ran out. A Chinese or Korean consumer product with live try-on could exist without being found here.
