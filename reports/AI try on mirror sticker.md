# Mirror stickers add touch, not clothes

A stick-on kit can make the mirror you already own respond to touch, but no transparent film can show you wearing your own clothes on your reflection, so the kit that works covers part of the mirror with a thin screen and a camera and shows a camera-made image of you, which is how every commercial try-on mirror already works. The blocker is optics, not effort: anything drawn on the glass sits at half the distance of your reflection, so it doubles in your vision and lands about 3 cm off your body for each eye, and a see-through emitter can only add light, so it can never hide the shirt you have on (est.). The one display you can actually stick on, transparent LED film, offers at best about 80,000 pixels over a half-metre-by-one-metre patch, dims your reflection behind it to 28–90% of normal, and costs ₹67,000–1.35 lakh for that patch from Indian sellers (est.). Touch is the easy half: an infrared frame around the mirror works on any glass, a corner laser sensor needs nothing on the glass, and a ₹12,000–58,000 capacitive foil, the only true sticker, fails behind a silvered mirror and is untested on its front. No product sells such a kit and the research found no 2024–2026 crowdfunding campaign for one, although mirror phone mounts, ₹2,500 touch LED mirrors, suction-mounted TVs and US$85–399 Chinese "magic mirror" panels show that every part exists. The most feasible design docks a budget 11-inch Android tablet or a 15.6-inch touch monitor on a steel plate bonded to the glass, or on magnets on a steel almirah, tracks your body on the device at about 30 frames a second to draw a lower-fidelity live preview of your garment, and fetches a photoreal still from a cloud API in 5–17 seconds for about ₹6–7. Kits run from about ₹500–1,500 (US$5–16) for a dock plus an app on your own phone, through ₹12,000–17,700 (US$125–184) with a bundled tablet, to ₹15,000–52,000 (US$155–540) for a 21.5–27-inch panel hung from a hook or cleat, against roughly ₹0.9–1.7 lakh for a full digital-mirror prototype (all est.). Letting only USB-C low voltage reach the mirror, and keeping anything stuck to the glass under about 1.3 kg with a tether, makes the kit safe; the largest risk is commercial, because buyers will compare it with a tablet on a stand and with Google's free try-on, so build and test the app before any hardware.

## No add-on try-on kit exists, but every part already sells

No product on sale in October 2026 sticks a screen and a camera onto a mirror you already own and dresses you in your own clothes. The research found no 2024–2026 crowdfunding campaign for an add-on mirror kit, only phone accessories, although Indiegogo's own search could not be loaded. Smart-mirror crowdfunding clustered in 2016–2019 and every campaign sold a whole mirror, the largest being Ayi's AI mirror with a 32-inch touch display, which raised **US$224,747 from 663 backers** in 2019 ([Kickstarter](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/ayi-smart-mirror/ayi-ai-powered-smart-mirror)). The full-mirror research found the same gap one level up: no home device has ever shipped virtual clothing try-on. Korea's HEYMIRROR, a CES 2026 Innovation Award honoree, stores a household's wardrobe and previews outfits as AI-generated images but has no confirmed camera ([CES](https://www.ces.tech/ces-innovation-awards/2026/heymirror-smart-dressing-mirror/)); LOOOK.AI's shop mirrors have rendered live, movement-following try-on since March 2026, but only of catalogue clothes ([Retail Technology Innovation Hub](https://retailtechinnovationhub.com/home/2026/3/19/loookai-goes-live-with-real-time-ai-powered-clothing-try-on-feature-for-smart-mirrors)); and Qualcomm's mirror at India Mobile Congress in October 2026 describes your outfit with an offline model and tries nothing on ([TelecomTalk](https://telecomtalk.info/ai-mirror-analyses-your-outfit-in-time/1012703/)).

The kit is new as a combination, and the evidence already points it toward a screen on the glass rather than a film over the reflection.

## Overlays can add touch but cannot dress your reflection

The question splits in two, and the halves have opposite answers: several overlays can make an existing mirror respond to a finger, but none can show you wearing something else on your reflection.

### Your reflection sits twice as far away as anything on the glass

Three physical limits apply to any see-through layer on a mirror, whatever it is made of. The first is depth. Your reflection appears as far behind the glass as you stand in front of it, so a garment drawn on the glass sits at half the distance of the body it should clothe. Your eyes must refocus by **0.33 dioptre at 1.5 m**, 0.5 at 1 m and a full dioptre at 0.5 m to switch between the two, and they converge about 1.2° differently at 1.5 m, more than they can fuse, so the drawn garment doubles whenever you look at your reflection (est.). A 2023 study of mirrors with screens built into the reflective surface found it "difficult to see both the reflection and the 2D display at the same time" and fixed it only with a lenticular layer and depth alignment ([3D-Mirrorcle](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13617)); a study pairing a see-through headset with a real mirror found users could not focus on both when content sat away from the reflection plane ([Augment Yourself](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.02884)). The second limit is having two eyes. Each eye's line of sight to the same point on your reflected body crosses the glass 31.5 mm from the other eye's at any distance, so a garment drawn for one viewpoint is about ±16 mm off on the glass for each eye, roughly **±3 cm on your body**, and holding even that much needs head tracking (est.). The third is that light only adds. Black is the absence of light and cannot be emitted, so wherever see-through content should be dark the viewer sees the real scene ([US patent 10001648](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10001648)), and content without occlusion looks "ghost-like" ([OST-HMD study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/268194699_Optical_See-Through_Head-Mounted_Display_with_Occlusion_Capability)). On a mirror an overlay can brighten a dark shirt but can never lay a navy kurta over a white one or hide a real sleeve, and dark try-on garments look see-through. The masking and dimming layers that would fix this ([US patent 11789688](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11789688)) need room behind a half-mirror, which a silvered household mirror does not have.

### LED film, the only stick-on display, tops out near 80,000 pixels

Transparent LED film does stick to glass, and it is bright enough. Installers apply it with its own adhesive to the inside face of shop windows and hide the controller in the frame, and they warn that coated or textured glass causes adhesion problems ([LikeLED](https://likeled.co.uk/led-window-film-guide/)). Resolution is the wall. The finest film anyone claims is **P2.5**, meaning 2.5 mm between pixels, from SWX and Muxwave, and Muxwave's own transparency figures for it contradict each other (vendor claims) ([SWX](https://swx-led.com/transparent-led-film/); [Muxwave](https://www.muxwave.com/transparent-led-film-101/)). P3.91 is the finest pitch commonly listed ([Alibaba](https://www.alibaba.com/product-detail/Transparent-LED-Display-Film-p3-91_1601055401915.html)), and LG's current self-adhesive film, the LAT140, has a 13.7 mm pitch, 1,728 pixels on a 655 × 492 mm panel and 53% transparency ([LG](https://www.lg-informationdisplay.com/product/led-signage/transparent-led-film/LAT140)). Over a 0.5 × 1.0 m patch, enough to cover the half-scale reflection of a 2 m person, P2.5 gives **80,000 pixels**, about quarter-VGA, P3.91 about 33,000 and LG's film about 2,600 (est.). A sharp eye at 1.5 m resolves about 0.44 mm, so even P2.5 shows a visible dot grid (est.); Planar's rule of thumb puts the distance at which P2.5 pixels blend together at 8.6 m ([Planar](https://www.planar.com/blog/2025/1/14/what-is-pixel-pitch-and-why-does-it-matter)). Drawn at your reflection's half scale, a shirt is about 120 film pixels tall at P2.5 and 22 at LG's pitch (est.): enough for colour blocks, not for fabric pattern.

The film also costs you the mirror. Reflected light crosses it twice, so your reflection behind it falls to the square of the film's transparency: about **28% of normal brightness** behind LG's LAT140 and 72–90% behind films claiming 85–95% (est.), and vendor "transparency" is often an open-area figure rather than true optical loss. The film's traces and LED packages show twice, once directly and once mirrored 5–8 mm deeper in the glass (est.). No source says which side of these films the LEDs face; window film is made to be viewed through the glass, so a film that emits toward its adhesive would fire into the mirror, and a vendor must confirm that a front-facing version exists. Power is high for a bedroom. Vendors quote 100–360 W/m² on average and 300–800 W/m² at peak (vendor claims) ([3CINNO](https://www.3cinno.com/media-mesh/transparent-led-film/); [TEC Displays](https://www.tecdisplays.com/Invisible-60-P62mm-Transparent-Film-Glass-Adhesive-LED-Window-Glass-Video-Wall-PG4711875); [HongWei](https://www.hongweiled.com/product/p6-p5-10-p8-transparent-film-led-display.html)), so the patch draws 50–180 W on average, which at the 5 V these films use means about 30 A at 150 W, and it could run 15–35 °C above room temperature at full rated power but only a few degrees above when dimmed for indoor viewing (est., order of magnitude only). Indian sellers list transparent LED screens for gluing to glass at **₹12,500–25,000 per sq ft**, mostly without stating pitch ([IndiaMART](https://www.indiamart.com/proddetail/transparent-led-adhesive-screen-26498987888.html); [IndiaMART](https://www.indiamart.com/proddetail/transparent-led-adhesive-screen-2855264459073.html); [IndiaMART](https://www.indiamart.com/proddetail/transparent-led-display-screen-2854991782248.html)), which puts the patch at ₹67,000–1.35 lakh before tax and controller (est.). Alibaba lists P3.91 film at US$449/m² for orders of 1–19 m², about ₹43,000 a square metre before shipping and duty ([Alibaba](https://www.alibaba.com/product-detail/Transparent-LED-Display-Film-p3-91_1601055401915.html)), and LG's discontinued LTAK140-GW panel listed at US$2,779 ([SHI](https://www.shi.com/product/46216506/LG-LTAK140-GW-LED-film)). At that price and resolution, LED film is a menu layer of big icons and colour swatches, not a try-on display.

### Every other display layer fails or hides the mirror

Transparent OLED comes only as rigid signage: LG's 30-inch touch model costs **US$7,500** at 1366 × 768 ([LG](https://solutions.lg.com/us/digital-signage/lg-30ew5tp-a)), its 55-inch panels cost from US$12,667 at 43% transparency ([LG](https://www.lg.com/us/business/digital-signage/lg-55ew5p-m)), and the 55-inch touch version passes only 33% ([AudioVideoNation](https://www.audiovideonation.com/lg-ew5tk-a-55-class-full-hd-transparent-touchscreen-oled-signage-display/)), which would leave the reflection behind it at 11–18% of normal (est.). A projector aimed at a mirror just throws its light back ([Linus Tech Tips forum](https://linustechtips.com/topic/1574151-projector-projecting-on-a-mirror-instead-of-a-projector-screen/)). Clear holographic film costs only US$35–45 a metre ([Made-in-China](https://windon1999.en.made-in-china.com/product/DEJrvCxhJjca/China-92-Transmittance-Holographic-Rear-Projection-Window-Advertising-Film.html)), but a low-confidence buying guide puts its gain at about 0.8–1.2 and says that even with a 4,000-lumen short-throw projector it "may appear dim unless ambient is near-zero" ([Alibaba guide](https://electronics.alibaba.com/buyingguides/rear-projection-film-for-glass-how-to-choose-right)). Switched off, PDLC "smart film" scatters light and can serve as a projection screen ([US patent 11768430](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11768430)), so a PDLC patch could flip between mirror and screen, but only by hiding the mirror in that zone, with a projector placed where you will shadow it; nobody has tested it over silvering, and its drive voltage was not found, which matters on a surface people touch. Transparent LCDs need a backlight behind them, which the silvering blocks ([US patent 9074749](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9074749)); colour e-paper is opaque and takes about 12 seconds per update ([The eBook Reader](https://blog.the-ebook-reader.com/?p=25028)); and two-way mirror film only works with a display behind clear glass ([Hackster](https://www.hackster.io/ben-eagan/smart-mirror-with-optional-alexa-874d43)), while one builder found its metal content interfered with the touchscreen ([MagicMirror forum](https://forum.magicmirror.builders/topic/19472.rss)).

That leaves one way for an overlay to show you in different clothes: make part of the mirror opaque and show a camera image of you with the garment drawn in, the "video see-through" approach every commercial try-on mirror uses. The cheapest opaque layer is a screen.

### Touch works three ways, and the true sticker is untested

Touch is the easy half because it does not have to look like anything. The route that fails is the standard smart-mirror build, a capacitive (PCAP) foil laminated behind the glass: WES Electronic says touch through glass needs non-conductive glass that "must not be metallized" ([WES Electronic](https://wes-electronic.de/en/news/blog/magic-mirror-touchscreen-behind-glass-or-mirror)), and a MagicMirror builder writes that foils "can't be used on glass with metallic coatings, which most of the mirrors have" ([MagicMirror forum](https://forum.magicmirror.builders/topic/2538/touch-overly-vs-smart-glass)). A foil stuck on the front is a different case, because your finger touches the sensor side and the silver sits 3–6 mm behind it, much as a touchscreen sits over an LCD's conductive layers. It needs a protective top sheet and a retuned controller, and the silver plane will weaken the signal and can add noise (est.); Visualplanet's advice to ground metal frames "to avoid any unnecessary interference" hints at the problem ([Touchfoil support](http://touchfoilsupport.blogspot.com/)). No foil maker addresses front mounting and no test result was found, though MagicMirror builders list a front-mounted capacitive layer among their workarounds ([MagicMirror forum](https://forum.magicmirror.builders/topic/16544/magic-mirror-with-touchscreen-display-monitor)). Foils are thin and, from Chinese makers, cheap, far below the US$1,977 a smart-mirror retailer charges for a 55-inch foil ([TwoWayMirrors](https://www.twowaymirrors.com/pcap-foil/)): CJTouch lists a 55-inch foil at **US$124**, or US$169 with a USB controller ([CJTouch](https://cjtouch.en.made-in-china.com/product/ZNlQiPLOkehb/China-55-Custom-Design-Nano-Capacitive-Touch-Foil-Panel-with-USB-Controller.html)); Tyco Touch's TF-55 is under 200 µm thick with 93% transparency at US$510 ([Tyco Touch](https://tycotouch.com/products/touch-foil-tf-55)); and Obeytouch's US$600 foil works through 0–12 mm of cover ([Obeytouch](https://www.obtouch.com/product/55-inch-high-sensitive-capacitive-touch-foil-nano-touch-film-obfilm550-160/)). That is roughly ₹12,000–58,000. This is the only true "sticker" for touch, and a bench test on a mirror offcut would settle whether it works.

An infrared frame is the safe choice. Its bars cast a grid of light just above the surface and sense a finger breaking it, so the silvering is irrelevant. A 43-inch frame costs from **US$60** on AliExpress ([AliExpress](https://www.aliexpress.us/item/2255799885691120.html)) to US$300 from Avacom, which claims ±1 mm linearity ([Avacom](https://avacomtech.com/infrared-touch-panel-ir-frame.html)), roughly ₹5,800–28,800, and most frames work as USB HID devices on Windows and Android without drivers ([Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/Chengying-Points-Multi-Touch-Infrared-Touch/dp/B07RQH8BT4)). DIY smart-mirror builders already favour IR frames because they cost less than foil ([TwoWayMirrors](https://www.twowaymirrors.com/diy-smart-mirror/)). The catches are shape and looks. Frames come in 16:9 screen sizes with raised bars on all four edges, and an almirah mirror roughly 35–40 cm wide and 1–1.2 m tall (est.) matches no stock size, so it needs a custom frame that was not priced; no mirror-specific report on IR frames was found either. A laser "radar" sensor in one corner needs nothing on the glass at all. Hokuyo's UST-10LX, sold for multi-touch walls ([Sensorlidar](https://www.sensorlidar.com/products/hokuyo-lidar-ust-05lx-ust-10lx-ust-20lx-ust-30lx-for-multi-touch-screen-interaction-and-robot-agv-navigation-obstacle-avoidance)), costs US$1,200–1,775, with a 10–12-month lead time at one retailer, but its ranging accuracy is only ±40 mm, so on-screen buttons must be large ([RobotShop](https://www.robotshop.com/products/hokuyo-ust-10lx-scanning-laser-rangefinder); [Clearpath](https://store.clearpathrobotics.com/products/hokuyo-ust-10lx)), and its scan plane must stay a few millimetres above and parallel to the glass, since a tilted beam hitting the mirror could create false touches, a case nobody has tested; Chinese interactive-wall kits priced at 290–900 in an unstated currency claim precision nobody has verified ([Alibaba](https://www.alibaba.com/product-detail/Gloview-Laser-Touch-Module-Interactive-Projection-1601218690733.html)).

Whole-mirror touch matters less than it first seems. You browse a wardrobe list at arm's length but try clothes on standing 1.5–2 m back, out of reach, so switching outfits mid-try-on works better with camera gestures, voice or a paired phone. In the docked-screen kit the tablet or monitor brings its own touchscreen, which turns a frame or foil into an optional extra for a "tap your reflection" menu rather than a requirement.

## A docked tablet and camera make the working kit

The kit that works has six parts: a dock that holds a screen on the glass, the screen, a camera, a processor, a power source, and a cloud try-on service for photoreal stills, with whole-mirror touch as an option. It shows a camera-rendered you on the screen while the rest of the glass goes on being a mirror.

### Screen size decides how big the rendered you looks

A 170 cm person standing 1.5 m from a mirror sees a reflection 31.6° tall. The same person filling 90% of a portrait screen's height beside it looks much smaller, and the ratio barely changes between 1 and 2 m (est.).

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6.7" phone | 7 × 16 cm | 17% | Any | Your own |
| 11" tablet | 15 × 24 cm | 26% | Any, including a 30 × 46 cm bathroom mirror | ₹11,499–13,999 |
| 15.6" touch monitor | 19 × 35 cm | 37% | Any; best fit on almirah doors | ₹6,090–13,990 |

Phones and 11-inch tablets suit "capture, then step closer to review"; a 15.6-inch screen works as a live second mirror from about 1.5 m; and 21.5–27 inches starts to feel life-size but weighs 3–6.5 kg and needs mechanical support (est.). The 15.6-inch USB-C touch monitors are the practical sweet spot. Acer's PM161QT lists at ₹9,888–10,299 ([Amazon.in](https://www.amazon.in/portable-touch-monitor/s?k=portable+touch+monitor)), Riitek's at ₹11,699 ([Riitek](https://riitek.in/products/touch-15-6)) and Leoxsys's at ₹13,990 ([Smartprix](https://www.smartprix.com/monitors/with-touch_screen)), with a no-name model at ₹6,090 in July 2024 data ([Smartprix](https://www.smartprix.com/monitors/portable-monitors-list)). They weigh about 1 kg ([Stuff](https://stuff.co.za/2025/08/12/acer-pm161qt-portable-monitor-review/)) and draw about 7.6 W, by ViewSonic's figure for a comparable model ([ViewSonic](https://www.viewsonic.com/edu/xtento_pdf/pdfPrint/product/template_id/2/product_id/75442/)), but their 200–300 nits look dim beside the reflection of a bright room, so keep them out of window reflections. A budget tablet bundles screen, camera, processor and battery for about the same money: the Redmi Pad 2 launched at **₹13,999** ([ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/!technology/xiaomi-redmi-pad-2-with-2-dot-5k-display-and-9000mah-battery-launched-in-india-enn25061901701)), and 2026 roundups list the Redmi Pad SE at ₹11,499–12,999 and the Galaxy Tab A9+ at ₹18,290–19,999 ([Croma Unboxed](https://www.croma.com/unboxed/best-tablets-under-rs-20000-in-2026)). A phone can drive a monitor only over USB-C DisplayPort Alt Mode, which Pixel 8 and later phones support ([Android Authority](https://androidauthority.com/google-pixel-8-display-port-3376141)), while Samsung lists only the Galaxy A90 5G among its A-series phones for wired DeX ([Samsung](https://www.samsung.com/latin_en/support/apps-services/what-is-samsung-dex)), so a kit cannot assume your phone can do it.

### The camera must sit low enough to see your feet

A camera stuck flat on glass cannot tilt down, so its height decides what it sees. To frame a 2 m-tall zone from 1.5 m away, a camera at about 1 m needs roughly **67° of vertical view**, falling to 53° from 2 m; mounted higher, it needs a tilting bracket or a far wider lens (est.). Lenovo's 300 FHD webcam, **₹2,375** on Lenovo's India store, has a 95° diagonal lens and a physical privacy shutter ([Lenovo India](https://store.lenovo.com/in/en/lenovo-300-fhd-webcam-gxc1b34793-387.html)). Rotated to portrait it covers 2.85 m vertically at 1.5 m, against 1.61 m in normal landscape (est.), so give it its own small bracket at about waist height, turned 90°. A tablet's front camera can replace it if its lens is wide enough: an assumed 80–90° diagonal lens would cover 2.0–2.4 m in portrait at 1.5 m (est.). No source gave the field of view or camera position of the Indian tablets listed, and whether budget tablets accept a USB webcam was not checked, so measure your target tablet before you design the dock.

### Phones can track you live, but photoreal stills come from the cloud

Following your movements is within reach of the device itself. Google's MediaPipe BlazePose ran at **20 ms per frame (Lite) and 25 ms (Full)** on the GPU of a 2018 Pixel 3, figures read directly from the project's documentation ([MediaPipe](https://github.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/blob/master/docs/solutions/pose.md)), and Google's ML Kit pose detector ran at about 30 fps on a Pixel 3 XL ([ML Kit](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/vision/pose-detection/android?hl=de)). No measurement on a 2024–26 mid-range phone or tablet was found, and the real risk is heat: pose, segmentation, garment drawing, rendering and the camera running for 5–10 minutes on a device stuck to glass with no airflow. Test on the cheapest target, such as a Redmi Pad SE, before committing. A Raspberry Pi 5 is now a poor processor box for this job. The 4 GB board costs US$110 after this year's memory-driven price rises ([Phoronix](https://www.phoronix.com/news/Raspberry-Pi-5-2GB-Price-77.50)), MediaPipe's 2023 Raspberry Pi port ran object detection at about 2 fps ([CNX Software](https://www.cnx-software.com/2023/08/21/mediapipe-for-raspberry-pi-released-no-code-low-code-on-device-machine-learning-solutions/)), and real-time vision needs a US$70–110 AI HAT+ on top ([Tom's Hardware](https://tomshardware.com/raspberry-pi/raspberry-pi-release-higher-performance-ai-hat-13-and-26-tops-variants)), so the box costs more than a whole tablet that brings its own screen, camera and battery (est.).

The rendering follows the two-tier design from the full-mirror research. On the device, the app flips the camera image left to right, as MediaPipe's own sample does "for a selfie-view display" ([MediaPipe](https://github.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/blob/master/docs/solutions/pose.md)), finds your body with pose tracking and MediaPipe's segmenter, whose classes include **clothes** but whose masks are not pixel-perfect ([MediaPipe model card](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-assets/Model%20Card%20Multiclass%20Segmentation.pdf)), and then either stretches a cut-out photo of your garment over your torso and limbs or recolours what you are wearing, at about 30 fps. The stretched garment follows you but looks like a sticker under strain and has nothing to show when you turn round; recolouring keeps real folds and shadows, as Uniqlo's 2012 mirror did for one jacket, though for home garments it is an untested idea. When you hold a pose for two seconds, one frame goes, with your consent, to a cloud try-on API. FASHN charges **US$0.075 per image** on demand and takes 5–17 seconds ([FASHN pricing](https://help.fashn.ai/plans-and-pricing/api-pricing); [FASHN docs](https://docs.fashn.ai/api-reference/tryon-v1-6)), and Google's Vertex AI Virtual Try-On costs about US$0.06 by third-party trackers ([CloudPrice](https://cloudprice.net/models/google-virtual-try-on-1)), so a still costs about ₹5.8–7.2. Live photoreal video exists in shops at about US$0.05 a second ([Genlook](https://genlook.app/blog/decart-ai-lucy-2-realtime-virtual-try-on)), roughly US$900, or ₹86,000, a month for ten minutes a day (est.), and in research such as LiveVVT's claimed 22.39 fps on undisclosed hardware ([alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.26714)), so "real time, following your movements" means preview quality on a kit in 2026. The licensing traps carry over unchanged: the best-known open try-on models, such as IDM-VTON and CatVTON, bar commercial use ([IDM-VTON](https://github.com/yisol/IDM-VTON); [CatVTON](https://github.com/Zheng-Chong/CatVTON)); FASHN VTON v1.5 is Apache-2.0 ([FASHN VTON](https://github.com/fashn-AI/fashn-vton-1.5)), although its bundled human parser carries a non-commercial licence and must be replaced ([fashn-human-parser](https://github.com/fashn-AI/fashn-human-parser)); and Ultralytics YOLO is AGPL-3.0 ([Ultralytics](https://github.com/ultralytics/ultralytics/blob/main/LICENSE)), while MediaPipe is permissively licensed. Lock the camera's exposure and white balance in the app, because automatic correction shifts garment colours, and treat your real reflection beside the screen as the colour reference.

### One dock design fits almirah, wardrobe and wall mirrors

India has no standard mirror sizes, but listings cluster: 24 × 42-inch and 30 × 30-inch wall mirrors, 12 × 18-inch bathroom pieces and 5-foot full-length mirrors ([IndiaMART](https://m.indiamart.com/impcat/wall-mirror.html); [IndiaMART](https://m.indiamart.com/impcat/mirror-stand.html)). A Godrej Slimline two-door almirah measures 194.8 × 89.9 cm ([Pepperfry](https://www.pepperfry.com/product/slimline-metal-2-door-almirah-in-pacific-blue-colour-by-godrej-interio-1809155.html)), and a government catalogue specifies a 1,980 × 920 mm almirah with a 25 mm mirror border ([GeM](https://bidplus.gem.gov.in/bidding/bid/showCatalogue/NUY7hnpZr0bP2PNLWk_K3Nq2D7aSD1OrYr20T4eMdpE)), which leaves a door mirror roughly 35–40 cm wide and 1–1.2 m tall (est.). A 15.6-inch screen in portrait fits every type, including almirah doors and small bathroom mirrors; 21.5 inches fits almirah, dressing-table and full-length mirrors but dominates an almirah; and 27 inches fits only full-length, dressing-table and large wall mirrors (est.). A mirror only half your height shows your whole body ([University of Illinois](https://van.physics.illinois.edu/ask/listing/1936)), so the useful band for users 160–180 cm tall runs roughly 0.75–1.75 m off the floor; put the screen at the side of that band, about 1.1–1.6 m up and clear of your face (est.). On a narrow almirah mirror the screen and your reflection compete for width: a 15.6-inch screen about 21 cm wide leaves 14–19 cm of glass, less than the 20–23 cm that adult shoulders span at half scale (est.). Make the screen lift off its dock, or mount it on plain steel beside the mirror where the almirah has some.

## USB-C power and a one-kilogram limit keep the kit safe

### Only USB-C low voltage should reach the mirror

Electrical safety is simpler for a kit than for a full mirror, because a kit need carry no mains at all. India's safety standard for this class of equipment is now **IS/IEC 62368-1:2023**, brought into BIS's Compulsory Registration Scheme by a 7 November 2025 notification, with the older standards accepted until 1 November 2028 ([SGS](https://www.sgs.com/en-no/news/2026/04/gma-update-april); [Matrix Test Labs](https://matrixtestlabs.com/blog/meity-announces-migration-to-isiec-62368-12023-a-major-shift-for-electronics-it-product-compliance)). It lets ordinary people touch only "ES1" circuits, at no more than **60 V DC or 30 V rms** ([Delta Electronics](https://psu.deltaww.com/de/industry-know-how/what-is-the-difference-between-selv-pelv-and-es1-in-ac-dc-power-supplies)). USB-C chargers deliver 5–20 V on their standard profiles (background knowledge the research did not verify), so a kit fed by a USB-C cable from a wall adapter keeps everything touchable inside ES1, provided the adapter's insulation between mains and output holds. Buy that assurance rather than build it. Power adapters and power banks are compulsory BIS registration categories ([Seyecs](https://blogs.seyecs.com/bis-certification-for-electronic-products/); [eChai](https://echai.ventures/d2c/d2c-product-licenses/bis-crs-mark-electronics)), 65 W USB-C GaN adapters sell for ₹1,419–2,499 in India ([Smartprix](https://www.smartprix.com/mobile_chargers/price-1000_to_1500); [Ambrane](https://ambraneindia.com/products/ambrane-raap-h65)), and you can look up an adapter's registration number on the BIS portal and print it in your manual. A tablet kit inherits all of this from the tablet and its own charger.

On a swinging almirah or wardrobe door, mains never goes on the door. Run the tablet on its battery and charge it off the door, or bring a coiled USB-C lead in along the hinge side with a service loop; a BIS-listed power bank on the door is a fallback that adds 300–450 g and a lithium cell (est.), so do not sell your own battery pack in version 1. The manual should recommend a socket protected by a 30 mA RCCB, but the kit must not depend on one: Schneider calls such devices "additional" protection because the other measures "cannot be regarded as being infallible" ([Schneider Electric](https://download.schneider-electric.com/doc/CA908066E/CA908066E%20%28web%29.pdf)). Dim and sleep the screen when nobody stands in front of it, which cuts heat and power together.

LED film, if you use it anyway, raises the stakes. Its 5 V feed draws tens of amps for a half-square-metre patch at full brightness (est.), so undersized connectors and ribbon tails become the likely fire point; run it dimmed from current-limited, fused supplies. Ask for the flame-test report on its plastic substrate: one vendor claims a "V1" rating ([DOIT VISION](https://www.doitvision.com/product/led-film/)), clear flame-retardant polyester rated VTM-0 exists ([Mylar](https://mylar.com/news/2020/dupont-teijin-films-introduces-flame-retardant-clear-polyester-film/)), and general-purpose clear polyester is only VTM-2 ([Toray](https://www.films.toray/en/ul/)). LG restricts its film to indoor glass at 0–45 °C ([LG UK](https://www.lg.com/uk/business/commercial-display/led-signage/indoor-led/ltak140-gu/)), and no film found carried a moisture rating. Keep every version out of bathrooms at first: wiring rules require IPX4 equipment in the zones near a bath or shower and 30 mA RCDs on every circuit ([Schneider Electric wiki](https://www.electrical-installation.org/enwiki/Bathroom_electrical_installation)), and none of the consumer tablets or portable monitors found carries an IP rating, so limit bathrooms to the phone-only tier, with the phone taken away after use.

### Tape and magnets can carry a tablet, not a TV

The mechanical rule is to stick about a kilogram to the glass and hang anything heavier. 3M designs its VHB foam tape for dead weight at 0.25 psi, which is 4 square inches of tape per pound with a safety factor above 5, and warns that the foam creeps under long-term load ([3M VHB design guide](https://multimedia.3m.com/mws/media/1400379O/3m-vhb-tape-design-guide.pdf)). That works out to about **57 cm² of tape per kilogram** (est.), so a 1 kg monitor needs four 25 × 60 mm pads, while a 5–6.5 kg 21.5–27-inch panel needs 284–370 cm² and would also load the mirror's own clips or mastic, which were never sized for it (est.). VHB 4910 costs ₹1,069–1,224 for a 12 mm × 11 m roll in India ([IndiaMART](https://m.indiamart.com/items/3m-vhb-tape-4910-s259637.html)). It is effectively permanent: removal takes fishing line, heat and solvent, with mixed results on glass ([Hunker](https://www.hunker.com/13415419/how-to-remove-a-vhb-tape)), so ship a removal kit and say so on the box. Renters can use Command picture strips, which list glass as a surface ([Officeworks](https://www.officeworks.com.au/shop/officeworks/p/command-picture-hanging-strips-white-16-pack-3m00335347)) and hold 5 kg on four medium pairs ([Screwfix](https://www.screwfix.com/p/command-self-adhesive-picture-hanging-strips-medium-12-pack/277ax)), but users report failures within days even on suitable surfaces ([ShopSavvy](https://shopsavvy.com/reviews-tldr/command-heavyweight-picture-hanging-strips)), so size them at four times the device weight. Suction cups hold by pressure difference and slowly leak air ([arXiv 1906.01459](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.01459)), which makes them fine for a phone and wrong for a screen.

The best mount is a dock: bond a thin steel plate to glass cleaned with isopropyl alcohol, let the screen attach magnetically, carry its weight on a lip or hook, and add a short tether to the frame or door. Steel almirahs can skip the adhesive. Godrej sells its almirahs in CRCA cold-rolled steel ([Bajaj Finserv](https://www.bajajfinserv.in/hindi/bmall/godrej-interio-2-door-crca-steel-almirah-wardrobe-30161803sd01019-blush-red/p/98364)), so magnets can grip the steel around the mirror, but the government catalogue's mirror frame is 0.8 mm sheet ([GeM](https://bidplus.gem.gov.in/bidding/bid/showCatalogue/NUY7hnpZr0bP2PNLWk_K3Nq2D7aSD1OrYr20T4eMdpE)) and magnet pull ratings assume thick, flat steel and fall on thinner sheet ([K&J Magnetics](https://www.kjmagnetics.com/blog/steel-thickness-and-magnetic-fields)), so test the pull on your own door. Wooden wardrobe doors have a weight budget: Blum specifies two hinges for 4–6 kg doors and three for 6–12 kg, for doors up to 600 mm wide ([Blum](https://ea.blum.com/en/number-of-hinges/)), and another maker's hinge guidance excludes accessories such as mirrors ([Lowe's](https://pdf.lowes.com/productdocuments/3a6f24c9-c094-4807-94b1-ca197bdb3974/65119808.pdf)), so keep the added mass under about 1 kg (est.). The tape never touches the silvering, which is on the back; the danger to the silver comes from solvent-based adhesives and acetoxy silicones that attack the backing ([EngineerFix](https://engineerfix.com/how-to-glue-a-mirror-to-a-wall-safely-2/); [Hranipex](https://www.hranipex.com/en/product/h-silicone-mirror-transparent-neutral-silicone-sil1/)), so clean round the edges with alcohol or citrus remover, and never load an acrylic stick-on mirror. Heat is a minor issue: a fanless tablet's back averaged about 42 °C after 30 minutes of heavy load, with spots near 48 °C ([Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Should-tablets-have-fans-Analyzing-tablet-PC-performance.446073.0.html)), while float glass with as-cut edges tolerates a 35 °C temperature difference ([Saint-Gobain](https://www.saint-gobain-glass.co.uk/document/the-resistance-of-glass-to-thermal-stress)), leaving a local difference of only 10–20 °C on glass at Indian room temperatures (est.). The serious hazard is a screen falling near a child. US emergency departments treat about **17,800 people a year** for furniture, TV and appliance tip-overs, 44% of them children, and TVs were involved in 47% of 217 tip-over deaths from 2013 to mid-2023 ([CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/Newsroom/News-Releases/2024/Ahead-of-Sundays-Game-CPSC-Reminds-Families-to-Tackle-the-Deadly-Hazard-of-TV-and-Furniture-Tip-Overs)). A 1 kg tablet is far lighter than a TV, but a tether, rounded corner bumpers and short, clipped cables are cheap insurance.

### The camera brings registration questions and privacy duties

Whether the kit itself needs BIS registration depends on what you bundle. "Smart mirror" is not a named category in the compulsory registration lists, and BIS had to issue explicit guidance before smart and AR glasses were placed, each needing its own registration ([Seyecs](https://blogs.seyecs.com/bis-certification-for-electronic-products/); [CliniExperts](https://cliniexperts.com/regulatory-update/guidance-on-product-category-under-compulsory-registration-scheme-crs/)). A passive dock contains no electronics, so the scheme should not touch it (an inference; confirm it with BIS). A retail tablet already carries its own registration, since tablets are in the scheme and moving to the 2023 standard ([Seyecs](https://blogs.seyecs.com/bis-crs-registration-for-laptop-notebook-tablet/)). Whether a bundle of registered parts sold as one kit needs its own registration was not researched, so ask BIS in writing before you sell one. A kit with your own processor box or panel and Wi-Fi becomes a new electronic product that needs IS/IEC 62368-1:2023 testing at a BIS-recognised lab; the full-mirror research also flagged wireless type approval and e-waste producer registration but could verify neither, and found no Indian fees for any of these steps.

Privacy is the heavier duty, because the camera faces a spot where people change clothes. India notified the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules on **13 November 2025** ([PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2190014&reg=3&lang=2)); most obligations, including notice, consent, security safeguards, breach reporting and user rights, apply from about **May 2027** ([EY India](https://www.ey.com/en_in/insights/cybersecurity/transforming-data-privacy-digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025)), and breaches must be reported within 72 hours ([Cy5](https://www.cy5.io/blog/dpdp-rules-2025-complete-compliance-guide-cloud-security/)). A kit launching in 2027 must comply from its first day. The defensible design runs the live loop on the tablet, holds frames in memory and never stores them, sends a photo to the cloud renderer only after explicit opt-in for each render, deletes it soon after, never uses customer images for training, and picks users by profile or PIN rather than face recognition, which counts as biometric processing. If frames never leave the device, the company never holds them, which shrinks its duties to account data and the renders users choose to send, a point that still needs legal confirmation. Tablets lack the physical lens shutter that a ₹2,375 webcam ([Lenovo India](https://store.lenovo.com/in/en/lenovo-300-fhd-webcam-gxc1b34793-387.html)) and Amazon's Echo Show 5 ([Amazon India](https://www.aboutamazon.in/news/devices/echo-show-5-3rd-gen-india-launch)) both have, so build a sliding lens cover into the dock, and add a parental mode that blocks cloud uploads by default.

## Build the app first, the dock second and the big screen last

The most feasible version 1 is a tablet dock. A thin steel plate bonded with VHB tape near the edge of the glass at about 1.1–1.6 m, or magnets on a steel almirah, holds an 11-inch Android tablet in portrait on a lip, with a short tether to the frame or door and a sliding cover over the lens. If the tablet's own camera cannot frame you, a 95° webcam with a shutter sits rotated on its own bracket at about 1 m. The app flips the image like a mirror, tracks your pose and clothes on the tablet, draws your chosen garment over you or recolours what you wear at about 30 fps under a "preview" label, and, when you hold a pose for two seconds, sends one frame with your consent to a cloud try-on API and shows the photoreal still 5–17 seconds later. The tablet runs on its own battery and charges off the door, or from a BIS-registered USB-C adapter at the wall socket, and nothing on the mirror carries mains. Touch comes from the tablet's own screen, with an IR frame or a front-mounted foil only as a later option. Build it in stages, each of which can kill the idea cheaply.

| 3. Android app and tier 1 dock | Port the live loop to a tablet; add wardrobe capture; measure frame rate and heat on a Redmi Pad SE-class tablet over 10 minutes on the glass | ₹11,500–14,000 for a test tablet | 30 fps holds without throttling, and testers come back weekly without reminders |
| 4. Tier 2C pilot | 10–20 homes with almirah, wardrobe and wall mirrors; magnet and VHB docks; pull and door-slam tests at four times device weight | ₹12,000–17,700 per home | No creep or falls after weeks, and people reach for it before their phone |
| 5. Productise | Written BIS category ruling, DPDP compliance before May 2027, licence audit, patent clearance; tier 3 only if pilots ask for it | Not costed | Someone pays |

Stage 1 answers the riskiest technical question at your own mirror for the price of a monitor and a webcam, and FASHN's minimum purchase of US$7.50 for 100 images ([FASHN pricing](https://help.fashn.ai/plans-and-pricing/api-pricing)) covers the photoreal tests. Stage 2 settles the one open hardware question: if a foil works on the front of a silvered mirror, a true touch sticker becomes a ₹12,000–16,000 add-on; if it does not, IR frames remain the answer for whole-mirror touch. The demand checks from the full-mirror plan, a survey of young Indians and a one-to-two-week dressing diary, cost only your time and belong before stage 4.

The market risk shrinks with the price but does not vanish. Every close analogue failed. Amazon's US$199.99 Echo Look stopped working in July 2020 after Amazon moved its style features into its Shopping app ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/05/29/echo-looks-will-cease-functioning-in-july-as-amazon-discontinues-the-camera)), Lululemon paid US$500 million for the MIRROR fitness mirror ([Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/digital/lululemon-acquires-at-home-fitness-brand-mirror-for-500-million/)), wrote off roughly that much and stopped selling it in December 2023 ([Lululemon 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1397187/000139718724000010/lulu-20240128.htm)), Naked Labs' body-scanning mirror became useless when its service ended on 31 October 2023 ([Naked Labs](https://nakedlabs.com/)), and Google closed its Doppl try-on app after about ten months ([Google Labs Help](https://support.google.com/labs/answer/16537062?hl=en)). A kit escapes two of those failure modes, because it costs a fraction of a ₹1 lakh mirror and a tablet keeps working as a tablet if your service dies. It sharpens the third, the drift of features into free phone apps: the closer the kit gets to an app plus a ₹500 dock, the easier it is to copy. Google's try-on has been free in India since **3 December 2025** ([Google India blog](https://blog.google/intl/en-in/products/virtual-apparel-try-on-tool-comes-to-india/)), Whering raised US$7 million in July 2026 with 10 million users, partly to add try-on ([Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2026/07/07/whering-lands-7m-as-digital-wardrobe-platform-reaches-10m-users/)), and any wardrobe app could add a camera "mirror mode" for a tablet on a stand. Your defensible ground is what generic apps skip: hands-free, full-body capture at the moment of dressing, your own wardrobe, and Indian occasion wear, where one seller-tools vendor says Google's try-on is not offered on saree and lehenga listings (vendor claim, unverified) ([Ai Vastra](https://aivastra.com/how-google-virtual-try-on-is-changing-fashion-shopping-in-india/)). That is why stage 1 includes a saree and a dupatta, the hardest garments for generative try-on. Before selling, also clear the two patent families the full-mirror research found, ACI Holdings' US 12,426,728 on smart mirrors that generate and display clothing recommendations ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12426728)) and Snap's US 11,880,947 on real-time upper-body garment exchange ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11880947)); nobody has read their claims, so get a freedom-to-operate opinion.

## Conclusion

The physics that rules out a transparent sticker also hands the kit its most useful feature. A full digital mirror replaces your reflection with video, and a phone app has no reflection at all, but a screen docked beside the glass leaves your real reflection, in true colour and true fit with no lag, next to a rendered alternative. The honest product is therefore a side-by-side comparison of what you are wearing and what you could wear, and that lowers the bar for the AI: the rendered you does not have to pass for a reflection, only to make it easier to choose between two options you can see at once.

The kit also turns a hardware venture into a software one. Its electronics are a tablet the buyer may already own and its only unique part is a dock worth a few hundred rupees, so the hardware risks that sank MIRROR and stranded Naked Labs' owners mostly disappear, and so does any hardware moat. What is left to defend is the app: the wardrobe it holds, the full-body picture it captures at the moment of dressing, and how well it handles Indian occasion wear. The evidence that decides the venture will come from whether people open that app at their mirror every week, not from a better film or a bigger screen.
